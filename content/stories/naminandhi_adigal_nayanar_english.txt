Chapter 01 - Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal's Periya Puranam, Part 527, Thirunindrasarukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. In the Periya Puranam, following the Puranam of Thiruneelakkar Nayanar, is the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. First, we shall briefly contemplate the history of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, and then delve into it in detail through the verses of Sekkizhar Peruman.

Among the prosperous towns in the Chozha (Chola) kingdom, one such town is Emaperur. This town is renowned for its inhabitants who live according to the Vedic principles. In this sacred place, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, a great ascetic who embodied unity in Saivism (Saiva Neriyil Oruimappadu), was born into a Brahmin family. He was like the fire nurtured by Vedic rituals. He firmly believed that the sacred ash (Thiruneetru) was the ultimate truth (Meipporul). He excelled in worshipping Lord Shiva day and night, attaining a state of bliss that never left his consciousness. From Emaperur, he would travel to Thiruvarur, considering the worship of Lord Puththidam Konda Nadhar and Lord Thiyagarajar to be the most excellent of all benefits. He would visit and worship them daily.

One day, after worshipping Lord Vanmeeganathar at Thiruvarur Poongkoil (flower temple) and living there for some time, he reached the outer courtyard. He then entered the Thiruaranneri Thirukkoil (temple of sacred righteousness) and worshipped there. He performed many services there. He wished to light many lamps there at night. It was the time when day had ended and evening had arrived. If he were to return to his Emaperur to bring the necessary items for lighting the lamps, it would take a long time, causing a significant delay. Therefore, he decided to procure ghee for the lamps from a house in Thiruvarur itself. He went to a nearby house in Thiruvarur, but it was a Jain house, where followers of the Jain religion lived. Unaware of this, he entered the house and asked for ghee to light lamps in the Shiva temple. They replied, "Does your Lord, who holds fire (Agni) in his hand, need lamps? There is no ghee here. If you want to light lamps, use water."

Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was deeply saddened and unable to bear their words. He returned to the Lord's sanctum and prostrated himself. At that moment, an ethereal voice (Asareeri) from the sky spoke, "Naminandhi, cast away your worries. Take water from the nearby pond and light the lamps." Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar descended. Chanting the name of Shiva, he took the water and came to the temple. He placed a wick in a lamp, twisted it, and poured the water from the pond onto the wick. He lit the lamp, and it blazed brightly. The world was astonished. Seeing the lamp burning with water, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was filled with joy. He lit lamps throughout the entire Aroor Aranneri temple. In front of the Jains who had mocked him with their words, he lit lamps with water for all the country to see. All those lamps burned until dawn. After ensuring they burned well by pouring water, he returned to his village at night. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar then performed Shiva worship, partook of sacred food (Thiru Amudhu), and slept. At dawn, after completing his puja, he went to Thiruvarur, visited the Aranneri temple, had a darshan of the Lord, performed sacred services (Thiruthondugal) throughout the day, and lit lamps at night.

During this period of performing sacred services for many days, the disturbances caused by the Jains were quelled by Thandiyadigal Nayanar. Those Jains, who lacked divine grace, were driven out of Thiruvarur. Thiruvarur shone with great glory. At this time, the Chozha Maharaja appointed Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar as the supervisor (Aththiyatchar) to arrange various offerings (Nipandhangal) for Lord Vanmeeganathar, ensuring they conformed to Vedic and Agamic rules. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar also arranged for the grand festival (Mahothsavam) of Panguni Uthiram for Lord Veethividanga Perumal in the month of Panguni.

During one such festival, Lord Thiyagarajar once graced the sacred place of Thirumanali. There, people of all castes and all communities came to worship Lord Thiyagarajar. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar also worshipped Lord Thiyagarajar. He was greatly delighted to witness the Lord's divine assembly (Thiruvoolakkam). In the evening, the Lord returned to the temple. After worshipping the Lord there, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar returned to his village. Upon reaching his village, he did not enter his house but slept outside. At that moment, Naminandhi Adigal's wife came and asked her husband, "Why are you sleeping outside the house? Come inside, perform Shiva Puja and Agnikaryam (fire ritual), and then sleep." Naminandhi Adigal replied, "Today, the Lord graced Thirumanali. I also went to worship Him there. People of all communities gathered there. Therefore, I have become defiled (Izhivu Vandhuvittadhu), and impurity (Theettu) has occurred. I need to take a ritual bath (Snanam) and perform atonement (Prayaschiththam) before I can enter and begin Shiva Puja. Please bring water for me to bathe." His wife quickly brought all that was needed.

Then, by the grace of the Lord, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar fell into a light sleep. The pure devotee slept there, meditating on the Lord's feet. In his dream, Lord Thiyagarajar appeared as if coming for his puja and revealed, "All those born in Thiruvarur are our ganas (divine attendants). You shall see this." Saying so, He disappeared. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar woke up. Considering his thought to be a fault, he immediately performed Shiva Puja. He then recounted all that had happened to his wife. After dawn, he went to Thiruvarur. Upon arriving, he saw that all those born in that sacred place (Thiruppathi) were shining with the form of Shiva (Sivasaroopiyam). He prostrated himself on the ground. Afterwards, they all shed their Shiva-like forms and returned to their previous appearance. Thus, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar witnessed the Lord revealing to him that all the people living in Thiruvarur were indeed endowed with the form of Shiva. He then prayed to the Lord, asking for forgiveness for the fault he had committed. Subsequently, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar moved to Thiruvarur and continued to perform his sacred services.

For a long time, he provided all that was necessary for the devotees of Shiva. For this, he was praised and glorified by Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar in his Thevara Thiruppathigam as the "pure gold" (Aani Pon) of the devotees. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar performed these sacred services in such a way that all worlds worshipped him, and he attained the divine abode of Shiva (Sivapadham). This is the brief history of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. Now, we shall contemplate the detailed history of the Nayanar, as presented in the Thogai Nool (summary work), Vagai Nool (classified work), and Viri Nool (expanded work). Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 - Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal authored Periya Puranam, Part 528, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. When concluding the Puranam of Thiruneelanakka Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman, bowing to the sacred feet of Thiruneelanakka Nayanar, sets the stage for the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in the concluding verse of Thiruneelanakka Nayanar's Puranam, stating, "With his aid, we proceed to narrate the sacred deeds performed by Naminandhiyar, a Brahmin of two births, who held steadfast devotion to Lord Shiva, who raises the bull banner." Here, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is referred to as a Brahmin of two births (Irupirappu Udaiya Andhanar). Generally, this Nayanar is a Brahmin, and therefore, the state of "two births" is obtained through Vedic rituals. Thus, the second birth that occurs during the Upanayana (sacred thread) ceremony for Brahmins is generally indicated here as a Brahmin of two births.

However, according to the Shiva Agamas, the attainment of Shiva Diksha (initiation), which grants the right to perform Shiva Puja, also results in a "second birth." This is the teaching of the Agamas. The Kamika Agama states that after offering three oblations with the Svahanta Moola Mantra for the Shudras and other castes, one should then say, "May this soul become pure from the faults arising from impure thoughts and the faults of birth, speech, and body, and attain two births." This is a verse from the Kamika Agama. Thus, through Shiva Diksha, the four varnas (castes), including the Shudras, attain two births. Therefore, Naminandhi Adigal is referred to here as a Brahmin of two births. This is a note by Srimath Muthukumarath Thambiran Swamigal. Just as "Senngnayiru" (red sun) is a natural epithet, "Irupirappu Udaiya Andhanar" (Brahmin of two births) can also be taken as a natural epithet. Sekkizhar Peruman sets the stage here, stating, "As per the Thevaram (hymn) saying, 'Meditate with a focused mind,' I now begin to sing the deeds of such a devotee who does not direct his mind to anything else but the Lord's feet." Now, let us begin to contemplate the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in due order. First, in the Thirutthondar Thogai (collection of sacred devotees), a summary text, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Naminandhi Adigal, saying, "I am a servant to the servants of the revered Naminandhi."

This phrase appears in the fourth verse of the Thirutthondar Thogai, which begins with "Thirunindra Semmaye Semmaya Konda" (who held righteousness as righteousness). "Arunambi Naminandhi Adiyarkkum Adiyen" means, "I am a servant to the servants of the great person, Naminandhi, who is a revered Nambi." Thirunavukkarasu Peruman praises Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar as a great person whose character serves as a tool to measure the greatness of sacred service, calling him "Thondarkanai." Such is the uniqueness of our Adigal Nayanar, and therefore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises him as "Arunambi." "Nambi" means an excellent man. The Nayanar's name is referred to as "Nambi Nandi" and "Nandi" in the Thevaram.

Thus, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings "Arunambi Naminandhi Adiyarkkum Adiyen" in the Thirutthondar Thogai. Now, in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a classified text, in the 31st verse, Nambiandar Nambigal has composed a sacred verse about Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. The verse is: "Veda marikaraththu Arur Ararkku vilakku neyyai Theedhu seri aman kaiyar attavidathen punalal Edhamuruga varuga renrandru vilakku eriththan Nadhan ezhil Ema Perur adhipan Naminandhiye." The meaning of this verse is: "Naminandhi, the lord of the beautiful Ema Perur, lit lamps for Lord Shiva of Thiruvarur, who holds the Vedic deer in his hand, with water, when the wicked Jains, who follow evil paths, refused to give ghee." This is the verse composed by Nambiandar Nambigal about Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a classified text. Now, the elaborate text is presented. This section describes the historical narrative and character of the devotee named Naminandhi Adigal. In this Sarukkam (chapter) called Thirunindra Sarukkam, in chronological order, the history of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is the seventh.

Naminandhi is the sacred name of the Nayanar. "Adigal" is an honorific title for great people. Therefore, this Puranam is titled "Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam." Sekkizhar Peruman describes the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in 32 sacred verses. The Chola kings were rulers who governed this earth with a unique scepter. During the time of our Sekkizhar Peruman, the Chola rule was at its peak, having conquered many lands and established a vast Chola empire. The Cholas had even expanded their rule across the seas. They wielded a unique scepter, ruling without any opposition. Such was the land of the Cholas, the sacred land of Kaveri, the land of the Valavar, the sacred land of Ponni. The Kaveri river bestows its full benefits upon the Chola country, making it prosperous. In such a Chola country, in the sacred land of Kaveri, there is a town called Ema Perur. This town is surrounded on all sides by paddy fields and water tanks. In these, many red lotus flowers bloom. There are ponds with abundant water everywhere. Lotus ponds are situated between the fields. The Vedas are those that speak of the true essence that removes impurities. They speak of the truth that removes the root impurity (Moolamalam) which causes the disease of birth. Along with this, the derivative texts of the Vedas also speak of good conduct. They speak of good conduct. Thus, Ema Perur is a town where good people live, who constantly contemplate and practice the principles and conduct described in the Vedas and their derivative texts. By describing the prosperity of the country, the prosperity of the city, and the excellence of its people, and stating that the town is filled with virtuous Brahmins, Sekkizhar Peruman begins the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in the first verse.

Sekkizhar Peruman mentions here that Ema Perur is a town in the Chola country. It is currently known as Thirumappatru and Neypperu. It is said that Neypperu might have evolved into Thirumappatru. It is believed that the name Neypperu (meaning "ghee town") might have come about because it was the town of the person who asked for ghee to light lamps, and when he didn't get it, he lit them with water. There is an old Shiva temple here, and there is also a separate temple for Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. The very house where Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar lived has been converted into a temple and has been worshipped for many years. There, the idol of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, adorned with the sacred thread (Poonool) and a small pot of ghee on his shoulder, grants us a divine vision. The Shiva temple is revered by this Nayanar. This sacred place is located south of Thiruvarur, on the way to Thiruthuraipoondi. It was in this Ema Perur that Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was born and lived. Sekkizhar Peruman describes its glories in the first verse as follows: "Vayyam purakkum thani sengol valavar ponni thirunattu Seyya kamala thadampanaiyum sezhuneer thadamum pudai udaiththay Poytheer vaymai arumarai nool purindha seela pugazh adhanal Eydhum perumai en thisaiyum Eror Ema Perural" (The Chola kings, who rule the world with their unique scepter, in the sacred land of Ponni, have fields of red lotuses and abundant water bodies surrounding it. Due to the fame of its virtuous conduct, which is based on the faultless truth of the rare Vedic texts, Ema Perur attains glory in all eight directions). Sekkizhar Peruman composed this first verse, and we have contemplated its meaning. We shall continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 - Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal authored Periya Puranam, Part 529, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing, beautifully composing the second verse: "Maalai payilum thoranangal marungu payilum mani marugu Velai payilum punal parugu megam payilum madangal Solai payilum kulirndha irul surumbu payilum arumboogam Kalai payilum veda oli kazhuneer payilum sezhuneer sey." In this verse, the word "payilum" (abounding in/practicing) is repeated again and again, illustrating how abundant the described resources are. The figure of speech "sol porul pinvarunilai" (repetition of word and meaning) enhances the beauty of this verse. First, he sings "Maalai payilum thoranangal" (garlands adorning the archways). In the beautiful streets of Ema Perur, garlands are hung on both sides. Archways adorned with garlands are one of the ways to decorate a city. Therefore, he sings that there are archways with garlands in the streets of the city, and "marungu payilum mani marugu" (jeweled streets abounding on the sides). Next comes "Velai payilum megam payilum madangal" (clouds that have drunk from the sea, adorning the mansions). In Ema Perur, there are streets filled with mansions. Clouds are seen on these mansions. These are clouds that have drunk from the sea. As per Thiruvempavai of Thiruvachagam, "Munnikkadalai surukki ezhundhu" (having shrunk the vast ocean and risen), the sea water evaporates due to the sun's heat and rises as clouds. Such clouds, having drunk the sea water, hover over the mansions of Ema Perur. "Solai payilum kulirndha irul surumbu payilum arumboogam" (cool darkness abounding in groves, and bees abounding in fragrant flowers) is the next phrase. Cool, dark shade is found in the groves, and bees swarm around the areca nut trees. Next comes "Kalai payilum veda oli kazhuneer payilum sezhuneer sey" (Vedic chants practiced in the morning, and water lilies adorning the fertile fields). In the morning, Vedic chants are heard in that town. Vedas are practiced at other times as well, but they are especially recited in the morning. This describes the glory of the town. Water lilies bloom in the fields of that town, indicating its abundant water resources. Now, the third verse. In this verse, he sings about the water resources and field resources of the town, and similarly, about the glories of the Brahmins who recite the Vedas there. "Panaiyil vilaindha vennellin parappu meedhu pada cheyya Thunarmenkamalam idaiyidaiye sudarvittu ezhundhu thondruvana Punarven purinool avar velvi kalaththil punaindha vedhigai mel Manal ven parappin idaiyidaiye valarththa sendhadhu" (On the expanse of white paddy grown in the fields, soft lotus flowers, blooming in clusters, appear here and there, shining brightly. On the white sandy expanse of the sacrificial altars, adorned by Brahmins with sacred white threads in their sacrificial grounds, the red fire they nurtured burns brightly here and there). In the fields of Ema Perur, white paddy grows. Red lotus flowers bloom and shine brightly, rising up. The blooming of lotus flowers amidst the fields indicates an abundance of water. Thus, red lotuses bloom and appear amidst the white paddy fields. In that town, there are Brahmins who recite the Vedas. They spread white sand on their sacrificial altars and nurture the sacrificial fire on it. Just as lotus flowers bloom in various ways to offer oblations to various deities in the sacrificial grounds, the sacrificial fire they nurture burns brightly on the white sandy expanses in the houses of the Brahmins. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, comparing the two.

The town is filled with Brahmins. Therefore, when singing about the prosperity of that town, even the natural elements there reveal a profound and righteous meaning to Sekkizhar Peruman's divine vision. He explains this to us here. Thus, after singing about the glory of the country, the glory of the city, and the conduct of the people living there in three verses, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to sing about Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in the fourth verse. "Perumai vilangum appathiyil penum neetru saiva neriyil Orumai vazhi vazh andhanartham ongu kulaththinul vandhar Irumai ulagum eesargalul irainji etra petra thavaththu Arumai purivar Naminandhi Adigal enbar ayinar" (In that glorious land, there lived Brahmins who cherished the sacred ash (Thiruneeru) and followed the Saivite path with single-minded devotion. Among their exalted lineage came Naminandhi Adigal, who performed rare penance, worshipping the Lord in both worlds). In that glorious land, there are Brahmins who cherish the sacred ash and follow the Saivite path with single-minded devotion. The sacred ash Saivite path is a path that protects those who embrace it and is cherished by those who follow it. The specialty of the Saivite path is the sacred ash. Therefore, it is called the sacred ash Saivite path here. Such Brahmins, who understood this Saivite path as the path of unity and lived with a unified mind, considering it a firm goal, are referred to here as Mahasaivas (great Saivites) who received Shiva Diksha (initiation) according to Shiva Agamas.

Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Brahmins who lived in that town as Mahasaivas, not just those who followed the Vedic path. From such a lineage of Brahmins, Naminandhi Adigal was born, who constantly performed and cherished the rare penance of worshipping and praising the sacred feet of Lord Shiva in both this world and the next. He is referred to here as "Arumai purivar" (one who performs rare deeds). Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is one who constantly performs that rare penance, who always cherishes the sacred feet of Lord Shiva.

He lives in the town called Ema Perur. Now, the next verse: "Vaymai marainool seelaththal valarkkum senthee ena thaguvar Thuymai thiruneetru adaive meypporul enru ariyum thunivinar Samakandar seyyakkazhal vazhipattu ozhugum thanmai nilai Yama iravum pagalum unarvu ozhiya inbam eydhinar." (He is worthy to be called the sacred fire nurtured by the virtuous conduct of the truthful Vedic texts. He is determined to know that the pure sacred ash is the ultimate truth. He constantly worships the red feet of Lord Samakandar (Shiva) and attains unending bliss, day and night, without his consciousness ever leaving it.) Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is one who always nurtures the sacred fire according to the principles of conduct stated in the Vedas and the texts derived from them. He is so constantly nurturing that fire that he is said to be the fire itself. Now, he sings, "Thuymai thiruneetru adaive meypporul enru ariyum thunivinar" (He is determined to know that the pure sacred ash is the ultimate truth). The sacred ash purifies. "Suddhamadhu aavadhu Neeru" (The sacred ash is purity itself) is a sacred saying from Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is one who follows the sacred ash as the ultimate truth. He is one who constantly worships the red feet of Lord Samakandar. Samakandar refers to Lord Shiva. Lord Shiva is the one who revealed the Sama Veda. Although he revealed all the Vedas, he is particularly fond of the Sama Veda. Therefore, Lord Shiva is referred to here as Samakandar due to this special preference. "Samagana Priyan" (one who loves Sama music) is a sacred mantra of Lord Shiva. Thus, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is one who constantly worships the red feet of Lord Shiva, who revealed the Sama Veda.

There is a rule that one should worship once, twice, thrice, or four times a day, according to the respective initiations. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is one who worships in this manner. The Thevaram says, "If one does not gather flowers and eat before eating, if one does not go around the temple at the appointed time." The Thevaram also says, "See Kalayanallur, where devotees with melted hearts worship the Lord's feet in the morning and evening." Thus, the Agamas state how many times and at what times one should worship the Lord. However, there is no time limit for worshipping the Lord. The rule is that one should never forget him. Therefore, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is one who constantly cherishes the Lord, never forgetting him, due to the discipline of that consciousness. It is also implied here that the Nayanar remains in this state even during the night when everyone else is asleep. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Yama iravum pagalum unarvu ozhiya inbam eydhinar" (He attained unending bliss, day and night, without his consciousness ever leaving it). It is mentioned here that he always praises the Lord, day and night. In addition, Sekkizhar Peruman sings here, "Yama iravum pagalum." "Yama" means midnight. There is also an Agamic rule that those who have received abhishekam (anointing) should worship at midnight. According to this rule, it is also implied that Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is conscious of the Lord even at midnight. Since "unarvu ozhiya inbam" (unending bliss of consciousness) is also mentioned here, it is explained that he attains bliss in this state of consciousness during all the watches of the night. Sekkizhar Peruman first describes the general Vedic nature of the people living in that town. Then, he describes the specialties and nature of the Saivite path, according to the sacred ash tradition. After that, he beautifully presents Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar as one who shone with the glory of Shiva knowledge, combined with his Vedic and Saivite specialties, and whose conduct was always exemplary, where Vedic chants were practiced at all times. He shows us that the Brahmins in that town are those who live by the sacred ash Saivite path, a path of unity. Thus, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was born amidst such arrangements, as Sekkizhar Peruman beautifully illustrates.

Therefore, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praises Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in his Thevaram, regarding the greatness of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar mentioned in this verse. The sacred saying of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman is: "Thavi avanudan irundhum kaanaadha Thathparanaai Aavidhanil anju odakki Anganan enru aadharikkum Naviyal seer Naminandhi Adigalukku nalkum avan Koviyalum poovezhu kor Kozhili Emperumane." (He who grants to Naminandhi Adigal, whose tongue praises him as Anganan, having controlled the five senses within himself, the supreme being who cannot be seen even by those who are with him, is our Lord of Kozhili, where beautiful flowers bloom.) In this, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman has specifically praised Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. We shall continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 - Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal authored Periya Puranam, Part 530, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing, "Avvur nindrum Thiruvarur adhanai adaivar adiyar mel Vevvur agattrum Peruman than virai soozh malarthal panivuruvadhu Evvoodhiyamum ena kollum ennam udaiyar pala nallum Thevvur eriththa varaichilaiyar thiruppadhangal vananginar." (From that town, he would reach Thiruvarur. He believed that bowing to the fragrant lotus feet of the Lord, who removes the fierce affliction from his devotees, was the greatest gain. For many days, he worshipped the sacred feet of the Lord, who burned the cities of his enemies with a mountain bow.) "Adiyar mel vevvuru agattrum Peruman" refers to Lord Shiva, who removes the fierce affliction of ego (anava malam), which is a cruel disease, from his devotees. Ego is the cause of repeated births and all suffering. Therefore, it is called "vevvuru" (fierce affliction) here. Lord Shiva is the one who removes such ego from his devotees. "Gnanaththiralaay nindra Peruman nalla adiyar mel Oonaththiralai neekkum aduvum unmai porul polum" (The Lord, who stands as a mass of knowledge, removes the mass of flesh from good devotees, and that too seems to be the true essence) is a verse from Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram, composed for Thiruvannamalai. "Sarntharaik kaaththal thalaivar kadan aathal" (It is the duty of the leader to protect those who seek refuge) is the second illustrative venba (a type of verse) for the second adhikaranam (section) of the tenth sutra of Sivagnanabodham. According to these sayings, the Lord is the one who removes attachments for those who are one with him and perform his service. Such a Lord is enshrined in Thiruvarur. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar believes that attaining the Lord of Thiruvarur, who protects from rebirth, and bowing to his lotus feet, is the foremost of all attainments, the greatest of all gains. Due to his state of attaining unending bliss in consciousness, day and night, by worshipping, this divine intention arose in Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar by divine grace. Therefore, he would go from Ema Perur to Thiruvarur to worship the Lord of Thiruvarur. He would go from Ema Perur to Thiruvarur daily, worship the Lord of Thiruvarur, and then return to Ema Perur. He continued this practice for many days. The Lord enshrined in Thiruvarur is "Thevvur eriththa Peruman," meaning the Lord who burned the three cities of the enemy Asuras (Thiripuram). Therefore, considering the Lord who burned the three cities as one who can destroy the three impurities (mummalas), and believing that bowing to the fragrant lotus feet of the Lord of Thiruvarur, who removes the ego from his devotees, is the greatest gain, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar would go from Ema Perur to Thiruvarur daily, worship the Lord, and then return to Ema Perur. Next, the following verse: "Semporputtrin manikkach sezhunjothiyai nerthozhum seelam Thampatrraga ninaindhu anaindhu thazhndhu panindhu vazhndhu pondhu Amporpurisai thirumuntril anaivar pangor araneriin Namparkku idamang koyilin ulpukku vananga nanninar." (He approached, thinking that worshipping directly the radiant jewel-like light within the golden anthill was his sole refuge, and bowing low, he lived. Then, he entered the temple, which is the abode of the Lord, in the sacred Aruneri, located near the beautiful golden rampart, and worshipped.) Thus, one day, as he was going to Thiruvarur daily to worship the Lord, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to narrate an incident that occurred due to the Lord's divine grace. As usual, one day, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar went to Thiruvarur. There, he worshipped the Lord directly, who was like a radiant jewel-light residing in a red-gold anthill. The Moolavar (presiding deity) of Thiruvarur temple is Vanmeekanathar, the Lord who resides in the anthill. Therefore, the Lord enshrined in the Moolathanam (sanctum sanctorum) of Thiruvarur is named Vanmeekanathar. It is natural for a snake and a snake's gem, and the gem's light, to be in an anthill. Here, the anthill is the abode, and the Shiva Linga form, which is a symbol of grace, is within it, and the Lord, who shines as a mass of divine light, is within that. Sekkizhar Peruman describes Vanmeekanathar as "Semporputtrin manikkach sezhunjothi" (the radiant jewel-like light within the golden anthill). That is, the anthill in the Moolathanam of Thiruvarur is the abode of the Lord. The Shiva Linga form within it is the Murthy (idol). The Lord, who is a mass of light within it, shines as the Murthiman (the one who embodies the idol). The Shiva Agamas classify these as Asanam (seat), Murthy (idol), and Murthiman (the one who embodies the idol). The anthill is the Asanam, the Shiva Linga form within it is the Murthy, and the Lord, who is the Murthiman within it, shines as Sekkizhar Peruman explains as "Semporputtrin manikkach sezhunjothi." Due to the custom of worshipping such a Lord, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar came to the Thiruvarur temple. Sekkizhar Peruman describes how his intention gradually manifests as actions. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar prostrated himself on the ground. Thereby, he attained the fruit of life. Then, he came out from the sanctum of Thirumoolathanathar and went to a separate temple called Aruneri, located in front of the beautiful golden rampart. Thiruvarur Aruneri is a separate temple within the Thiruvarur temple complex. Thiruvarur is a large temple, and the Aruneri temple is a separate temple located in the southeast direction within it. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar came to this Aruneri temple in Thiruvarur. "Nanni irainji anbinnal nayapputru ezhundha kaathaludan Annalarai panindhu ezhuvar aduttha nilamai kurippinal Pannum thondin pangu pala payindru paravi viravuvar Ennil theepam ettratharku eduttha karuththin isaindhu ezhuvar." (He approached and worshipped with love, rising with intense devotion. He then began to perform various services, praising and spreading them. He rose with the intention of lighting countless lamps.) When Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar came to the Thiruvarur Aruneri temple, a divine intention arose in his sacred heart by the grace of the Lord. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar had a habit of coming to the Thiruvarur temple and worshipping directly for many days. But on this day, by the grace of the Lord, a divine intention arose in the Nayanar's heart. On that day, he worshipped Lord Purathanar, who resides in the anthill in Thiruvarur, with great love and prostrated himself. Then, he specifically entered the Aruneri temple and worshipped. This worship was not like before; it was an intense love that arose and flowed. Therefore, on that day, he did not stop at worship but resolved to perform many services there. The services performed for the temple include sweeping, decorating with garlands, and performing renovation work. But here, the Nayanar's heart desired to light many lamps. Lighting lamps is said to be a cause for knowledge. "Vilakku ittar peru sollin mey neri gnanamagum" (The reward for lighting a lamp is the knowledge of the true path) is a sacred saying from Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's Thevaram. Therefore, the Nayanar's heart desired to perform the sacred service of lighting lamps, which is a cause for knowledge. The time when the Nayanar thought of lighting lamps was evening, when the day was ending. He needed ghee to light the lamps. If he were to go to Ema Perur to bring ghee at this time, the day would be over, and it would cause a great delay. Therefore, he thought that it was impossible to go to his village and bring ghee. He thought of buying ghee from a house in Thiruvarur itself to light the lamps.

There are houses around the Kamalaalaya sacred pond, located north of the Thiruvarur temple. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar went to one such house, with the thought of needing ghee. However, the house Naminandhi Adigal entered was a house where Jains lived. Jains are those who have deviated from the true path. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar entered such a house where Jains lived, without knowing that it was a Jain house. The Jains there said, "Kaiyil vilangu kanal udaiyar thamakku vilakku migai kaanum ney ingu illai Vilakku erippeer aagil neerai mugandhu eriththal seyyum enru thiruththondarkku uraiththar theliyadhoru porulai poyyum meyyumam enum porul melkollum purai neriyar." (They told the sacred devotee, "There is no ghee here. If you wish to light lamps, you can light them with water. For the one who holds fire in his hand, a lamp is superfluous." Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse, describing them as "those who follow a flawed path, believing that an unclear object can be both false and true.") This is the tenth verse in the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. Jains hold the principles of Asti-Nasti (existence and non-existence). They believe that the same object can exist and not exist, can be both false and true. Asti means "is," and Nasti means "is not." Sivagnana Munivar has explained this principle held by Jains in the section on "Arugatha Matham" (Jainism) in the introduction to Sivagnana Padiyam. When asked if the Jiva (soul) exists or not before taking a body, they would say, "The Jiva exists, it does not exist, it cannot be said to exist or not exist, it exists and cannot be said to exist, it does not exist and cannot be said to exist, it exists and does not exist and cannot be said to exist." They would give seven types of answers, never giving a definitive answer. Therefore, this Arugatha Matham is called Anekantavada (the doctrine of non-one-sidedness). They would continue to define the Jiva in this manner without any conclusion. These Jains are considered to be outside the pale of the 24 religions established by Saivism. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman states in this verse that such Jains are "purai neriyar" (those who follow a flawed path), meaning they are "those who have not attained clarity about the true essence."

Furthermore, he mentions that they are Jains who follow a flawed path. Sekkizhar Peruman states this. Therefore, such Jains would not have the opportunity to understand the greatness of Naminandhi Adigal, the sacred devotee. They told Naminandhi Adigal, "There is no ghee here. If you wish to light lamps, light them with water." They told Naminandhi Adigal, the sacred devotee, to light lamps with water. They also said, "Kaiyil vilangu kanal udaiyar thamakku vilakku migai kaanum" (For the one who holds fire in his hand, a lamp is superfluous). They said that for Lord Parameswaran, who holds fire in his hand, a lamp is superfluous. Not only that, but they also said that if he wanted to light lamps, he should light them with water. Water has the property of extinguishing a burning lamp. Therefore, they contradictorily told him to light lamps with water. This clearly illustrates their principle of Asti-Nasti. Thus, it is implied here that the conduct of those who uphold such flawed principles will also be like this. Here, what they said was directed at the sacred devotee, but it was not merely said to the devotee; it was said to the Lord who resided in the devotee's heart. Therefore, the Lord's divine word is now about to manifest. We shall continue to contemplate that. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 - Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal authored Periya Puranam, Part 531, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, who went to a Jain house unknowingly to ask for ghee to light lamps, was disrespected by the Jains. If the Jains had simply said they would not give ghee for the lamps, that it would be against their religious principles, it would have been somewhat acceptable. However, the Jains insulted and humiliated him. This is an unjust act, an act against dharma. Hearing their insults and humiliation of Saivism, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was greatly distressed. He could not bear to hear their insults to Saivism and the devotees, and his mind was troubled. He did not consider their disrespect towards him as important, but they were insulting Saivism and Lord Shiva. Therefore, the Nayanar could not bear it. In the Thiruvaiyaru Sthala Puranam, the sixteen boons that Nandi Peruman received from the Lord are explained. Nandi Peruman first asks for such devotion that he cannot tolerate hearing insults to the Vedas or Saivism.

The rule is to punish those who insult Shiva. We see this in the histories of Shakti Nayanar Puranam, Viranminda Nayanar Puranam, and others. When it is not possible to punish those who insult Shiva, the rule is to immediately cover one's ears, chanting the name of Shiva, and leave that place. That is, one should not tolerate hearing insults to Saivism or the Vedas for even a moment. Therefore, one must either punish them or immediately leave the place, covering one's ears. Thus, hearing the Jains insult Saivism, and being unable to do anything about it, caused distress in the Nayanar's mind. The Nayanar, with great sorrow, left that place. With the conviction that Lord Shiva alone could provide a solution, the Nayanar entered the temple. Lord Shiva alone could provide a solution. What was the solution? The Lord should grant punishment for the Jains' evil words and a way to fulfill his desire to light lamps. That was the solution. Believing that Lord Shiva alone could provide that solution, the Nayanar entered the temple. He prostrated himself before the Lord's feet and made his plea. At that moment, a unique divine voice, a voice of unparalleled grace, arose from the exalted form of knowledge. The verse is as follows: "Vandha kavalai maatrum ini maaraa vilakku pani maara Indha marungil kulaththu neer mugandhu kodu vandhu ettrum ena Andhi madhiyam anindha piraan arulaal ezhundha murai Mozhi kelaa sindhai magizhndhu Naminandhi Adigal seivadhu arindhilal Sennimisai neerththariththa Piraan arulae sindhai seydhu ezhuvar Nannir poigai naduppukku nadhar naamam navindru ettri Annir mugandhu kondu eri appar koyil adaindhu agalul Munnir ulagam adhisayippa murukkum thirimeel neer vaarththar." (Two verses are arranged in sequence: "Change the worry that has come. Now, to perform the service of lighting lamps without ceasing, draw water from the pond nearby and light them." Hearing this divine word that arose by the grace of the Lord, who wears the crescent moon at dusk, Naminandhi Adigal's mind rejoiced, not knowing what to do. He rose, meditating on the grace of the Lord who bears water on his head. Entering the good water pond, chanting the Lord's name, he drew that water, ascended, reached the Appar temple, and poured water on the twisted wick in the lamp, astonishing the three worlds.) A divine voice (Asareeri) arose from the sky of knowledge as the Lord's divine word, addressing Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. That ethereal voice said, "Cast away the worry that has come. Now, to perform the service of lighting lamps without ceasing, bring water drawn from this nearby pond and light the lamps." The worry in Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar's mind was his inability to light the lamps; that was his first worry. Having to hear the Jains' mocking and insulting words was his second worry. Therefore, the divine word that arose by the grace of the Lord told him to cast away these two worries. The divine word of grace says, "It is difficult to change the worries of the mind unless one clings to the feet of the incomparable one," as stated in Thirukkural. Therefore, the ethereal voice first tells the devotee, who always clings to the Lord's sacred feet, "Cast away the worry that has come to you." The ethereal divine word continues, "Take water from this nearby pond and light the lamps." This incident takes place in the Thiruvarur temple.

Therefore, the divine word refers to the water in the pond inside the temple. It is believed that this refers to the Sangha Theertham, which is located west of the Devasiriyan Mandapam. It is believed to be the Sangha Theertham inside the Thiruvarur temple. That is why the divine word says "nearby." This pond is near the place where the Nayanar prostrated himself in front of the Lord of the Anthill. It is also said that there was a small pond in front of the Thiruvarur Aruneri temple earlier. Therefore, the divine word tells him to take water from this sacred pond and light the lamps. It was a divine word that arose by the grace of Lord Shiva. Lord Shiva, who wears the crescent moon at dusk, is the one who makes light shine in the time of darkness. Therefore, Lord Shiva, who wears the moon, granted him the ability to light lamps here. The moon, whose light was diminishing due to a curse, came and sought refuge at Lord Shiva's feet. Lord Shiva made him shine again. Similarly, Lord Shiva is now going to make the lamps burn with water, which has the property of extinguishing fire. This is the implication here. When the divine word suddenly arose from the sky of knowledge, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was bewildered, not knowing what to do. This was due to the sudden great joy. Therefore, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar rose, meditating on the grace of the Lord who bears water on his head. Lord Shiva is the Lord who bears the Ganga water on his head.

Lord Shiva is the Lord who bore the Ganga water, which came for the salvation of his ancestors. Therefore, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar rose, his mind completely filled with the grace of such a Lord, believing that even with this water, the lamp would burn by the grace of Lord Shiva. This is because when he prostrated himself, this divine word arose. Hearing it, the Nayanar rose, meditating on the grace of Lord Shiva. He went to the pond indicated by the divine grace. The water in that pond is good water because it is the water that serves as an instrument for the manifestation of divine grace, for the growth of the lamp's fire, for Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar's joy, and for the distress of the people of other religions. Therefore, it is good water. Thus, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, who always chanted the name of Lord Shiva, entered that good water pond and took water, still chanting the name of Lord Shiva. Those who begin a great task, by chanting the Lord's name, are said to be surrendering themselves to the aid of Lord Shiva's grace and beginning that task. Amarneethi Nayanar, when stepping onto the balance, chanted the Lord's name, the sacred five letters. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, when immersing himself in the sacred pond in the Kailasa mountain range by the Lord's command, immersed himself chanting the five letters. Pugazhchola Nayanar, when entering the fire, also entered chanting the five letters. Thus, we recall the divine histories of other Nayanars who did the same. Thus, chanting the Lord's name, he took the water, came inside the Aruneri Appar temple in Thiruvarur, and poured that water into an oil lamp, filling the lamp. He placed a wick in it and poured the water onto the wick, wetting it. He twisted the wick. Twisting the wick prevents it from burning too quickly and makes the lamp burn steadily, and helps the flame to catch fire quickly. Thus, he twisted the wick, moistened it with water, and lit the oil lamp. "Sothi vilakku ondru ettrudhalum" (And when he lit one lamp) comes in the next verse. Why did he light only one oil lamp with water, instead of many? Did the Nayanar doubt the divine grace? No, that is not it. The Nayanar knew well that divine grace could do anything and that what he heard as an ethereal voice was indeed the divine word. Therefore, the Nayanar entered the pond, chanted the name, took the water, came inside the temple, and poured water into the lamp. Yet, even if the ocean is vast, only the amount of water that fits into the vessel will be filled. Similarly, even if divine grace is vast, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar lit only one lamp, fearing whether he was worthy to receive it, and what was the extent of his worthiness. This is the nature of great people. Thus, he filled the oil lamp with water and lit it. That is, the Nayanar did this, astonishing all the worlds surrounded by this ocean. The ocean has the power to create, sustain, and destroy the world. Therefore, the ocean is called "munneer" (three waters) because of its three functions. At the same time, here, water acts contrary to its nature. Instead of extinguishing fire, it helps to burn fire. It helps to light the lamp. Therefore, it is also referred to as "munneer" here. Thus, when the Nayanar filled the oil lamp with water and lit it, it shone brightly and flared up by divine grace. Its flame rose high and burned well. Following this, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar lit lamps everywhere throughout the temple of Aruneri Appar, the primordial Lord. We shall continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal authored Periya Puranam, Part 532, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar lights lamps with water, astonishing this entire country. Therefore, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar lit lamps with water throughout the Thiruvarur Aruneri Appar temple, right in front of the Jains, so that they, the followers of external religions, would be bewildered and see the truth. According to this, he lit lamps with water so that everyone, including the followers of external religions, would know the truth and see it directly. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar poured enough water into all the lamps to keep them burning until dawn. He lit lamps everywhere, illuminating the entire temple, and poured enough water to keep them burning well throughout the night. After lighting the lamps in this manner, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar proceeded to his village, Ema Perur. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar's daily practice at home was the Atmaartha Shiva Puja (self-worship of Shiva). That is the duty of Atmaartha Shiva Puja. Coming to Thiruvarur and worshipping the Lord enshrined in the Poongoyil (flower temple) and Aruneri Appar in Thiruvarur is a form of Paraartha Puja (worship for others), which is a temple worship. Therefore, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was one who followed the rules prescribed by the Agamas, performing Atmaartha Puja at home and Paraartha worship at the Thiruvarur temple. Therefore, after returning home that night, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar performed his Atmaartha Puja. Thus, it is understood that Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar had a rule of performing Shiva Puja even at night. He could not stay in the Aruneri temple in Thiruvarur all night to pour water into the lamps. Therefore, he poured enough water into all the lamps to keep them burning until dawn and then returned to his house before the night ended. The rituals and pujas performed before midnight are considered to belong to that day, and those performed after midnight belong to the next day. Therefore, to perform the nightly puja, which was his duty for that day, without delay, he returned home before midnight that very night. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions this in the verse: "Nirayum parisu thiruvilakku vidiyum alavu nindru eriya Kuraiyum thagaligalukku ellam kolla vendum neer vaarthu Maraiyin porulai archikkum manaiyin niyathi valuvamal Urayum padhiyil avvirave anaivar panivutru varupattar." (He poured enough water into all the lamps to keep them burning until dawn, so that they would not diminish. He returned to his village that very night, performing his duties, so that the rule of worshipping the essence of the Vedas in his home would not be violated.) Now, the next verse: "Iravu sendru tham padhiyin eydhi manaippukku enrum pol Viravi niyama thozhil muraiye vimalar thammai arutchippu Paravi amudhu seydhu aruli palli kondu pularkalai Aravam anivar poosai amaiththu Arur nagarin meendu anaindhar." (He went to his village that night, entered his house, and as always, performed his regular duties, worshipped the pure Lord, praised him, partook of sacred food, and slept. In the early morning, he performed the puja for the Lord who wears the serpent, and returned to the city of Thiruvarur.) He thus reached his village at the right time and entered his house. As always, he worshipped the pure Lord that night. He partook of the sacred food for that night. Then, he slept. Thus, he completed all his duties that night, without delay, according to the rules and regulations, at the proper time. Then, the next morning, after completing his regular Atmaartha Shiva Puja, he began to perform the Lord's service. Therefore, the rule is that one should perform the Lord's services only after completing one's Atmaartha Puja. This Nayanar followed this rule without fail. Thus, having completed his Atmaartha Puja, and believing that worshipping the Lord of Thiruvarur was beneficial for the soul, he returned to the city of Thiruvarur, just as he had done for many days. Moreover, the joy of the divine grace he had received the previous day still filled his mind, and he wished to perform service again and immerse himself in the abundance of divine grace. Therefore, he returned to Thiruvarur. He worshipped Lord Purathanar, who resides in the Poongoyil in Thiruvarur, and Lord Thiyagaraja, and then entered the Thiruvarur Aruneri temple, circumambulated it, and worshipped. "Vandhu vanangi araneriyaar magizhum koyil valam kondu sindhai magizha Panindhu ezhundhu purambum ullum thiruppanigal mundha muyandru pagallam muraiye seydhu Maraiyanavar andhi amaya thariya vilakku engum ettri adipanivar." (He came and worshipped, circumambulated the temple where Aruneri Appar rejoices, and with a joyful mind, prostrated himself and rose. Thus, he came and worshipped, circumambulated the temple where Aruneri Appar rejoices, and with a joyful mind, prostrated himself and rose. Having completed his Atmaartha Puja early in the morning, the Nayanar performed sacred services throughout the day at the Thiruvarur Aruneri temple until it was time for his nightly puja. He performed the external services, such as sweeping, anointing with sacred paste, and performing weeding work, and also the internal services, such as lighting lamps, making garlands, and preparing items for sacred ablutions. He performed all these services diligently throughout the day, without delay. He performed what needed to be done first, first, and what needed to be done later, later, knowing the proper order and time. Similarly, that day too, when evening arrived, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar lit lamps with water everywhere in the temple. He continued to perform these services for many days. He performed all this as a regular discipline.

During this same period, the Jains were driven out of that town by Thandiyadigal Nayanar. We will see this history in detail in the Puranam of Thandiyadigal Nayanar. Thus, when the Jains were driven out of Thiruvarur, the Saivite path shone brightly in Thiruvarur, where the gods praised the feet of the devotees of Lord Thiyagaraja, the chief of the gods. The eternal Saivite path flourished, and glory, praised by the seven worlds, prevailed there. The Vedas are in the form of sound (Nada), and Nada is the form of Shakti. Therefore, the Vedas are in the form of pure consciousness (Chit Shakti), which is knowledge. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was well-versed in the Vedas. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was one who understood the ultimate truth of the Vedas and lived accordingly. He thus performed sacred service to the Thiruvarur temple. The king of that country heard about this and became a devotee of Naminandhi Adigal. We shall continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 - Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal's Periya Puranam, Part 533, Thirunindrachcharukkam, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam. Hearing about the sacred service that Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was performing, the king of that country became a devotee of Naminandhi Adigal. This was because Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was performing the sacred service of lighting lamps, and that too, by the grace of God, lighting lamps with water. Regarding the greatness of this sacred service of lighting lamps and the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, Pandit M. Kandiah writes the following introduction: "Among the sacred services in Shiva temples, the service of lighting lamps directly leads to spiritual knowledge (Gnanam). The joy gained from a lamp is multiplied tenfold by plastering (the floor with cow dung). If one offers good, fresh flowers, one will attain a pure heavenly abode. If one speaks of the reward for lighting a lamp, it is the true path of knowledge (Gnanam)." This truth is evident from the Thevaram verse: "The immeasurable hymns sung by the devotees are blessed by the Lord." Since the very nature (Swarupam) of Shiva, our worshipped deity, is to shine as light, and since a soul that has matured and attained true knowledge is known to be in the form of light, witnessing these two effulgent manifestations is a direct path to liberation (Mukti). Therefore, lighting a lamp, which is a symbol of these lights, in the presence of Shiva and worshipping Him is a means to attain Gnanam. The luminous nature of Shiva's Swarupam is mentioned in various places in the Thevaram. It is referred to as "the Lord who is a light within the red coral and white crystal that does not leave Thiruvaiyaru." It is also sung in the Thiruvachakam as "the great light without beginning or end," "the form that appears as light," and "the light, the flame, the lamp of encompassing light." In Thiruisaippa, it is sung as "the lamp of growing light" and "the pure one, the light within the light of the bright lamp, the light of lights." "He appears as the light within the light" is from Sivagnana Siddhiyar. "Seeing Murugan's form within the light of Omkara, one does not sleep" is from Kandar Alankaram. These sacred sayings are thus structured. Furthermore, true knowledge and the soul in a state of true knowledge will also be luminous. Therefore, the Thirumandhiram says: "Light the lamp and know the space; before the lamp, suffering will cease. Those who illuminate the lamp are the lamp that illuminates." Now, the Thirumandhiram also states that witnessing these two types of lights is the direct path to liberation: "He who can split the lamp, light the lamp, ignite the lamp within the lamp, and illuminate the lamp with the lamp, will attain the Lord of the ocean." This was blessed by Thirumooladeva Nayanar. Furthermore, in the Thevaram, it is stated: "If one looks with the fire of knowledge, kindled by the wick of intellect, with the ghee of understanding poured into the lamp of the mind within the house of the body, one can see the father of the youth who rides the elephant, the Lord who resides in the ocean." This Thevaram verse describes the internal process of knowledge (Gnana Thozhil Paadu) as the act of lighting a lamp. Therefore, it elaborates on its components: the lamp (Thagazhi), ghee, wick, and flame. Thus, it confirms that lighting a lamp in a temple, which is an external act performed with these same components, is indeed a means to attain Gnanam. This Thevaram verse supports this. In the previous Puranam of Thiruneelanakka Nayanar, it was stated that Shiva Puja is a means to attain Shiva's grace, and it was explained that even staying in a place is Shiva Puja. Similarly, lighting a lamp is a means to attain Gnanam and also a means to utilize the Gnanam thus attained. This is explained to us in the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar.

The sacred service of lighting lamps performed by Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is presented here as a means to enhance the experience of Gnanam. It is stated by Sekkizhar Peruman in the first part of the Puranam that Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar was already a great soul who had attained Gnanam. Therefore, to further enhance the Gnanam he had attained, the sacred service of lighting lamps was performed by him. By the grace of Shiva, Nayanar lights lamps by pouring water as ghee into the wicks, and they shine brightly. When Nayanar sees this, he perceives the beauty of the lamp as the flame of knowledge shining within his heart.

Sekkizhar Peruman sings that all those born and living in Thiruvārūr, who live by the principle that the purpose of life is to worship Shiva and His devotees without forgetting them, have attained Shivasaaroopam (the form of Shiva). Here, Sekkizhar Peruman does not say "all those born in Thiruvārūr," which would include everyone born there, but "all those born and living in Thiruvārūr." Therefore, even if one is born in Thiruvārūr, if one does not live a life of worshipping Shiva and His devotees without forgetting them, they have not attained the purpose of life. Therefore, they would not fall within this category. Thus, all those born and living in Thiruvārūr who follow the path of Shiva appear to Nayanar's eyes as having attained Shivasaaroopam. Shivasaaroopam is a state that comes as a result of Shivayoga. It is a stage before great souls who have mastered Shivayoga attain Gnanam and Shivasaayujyam (union with Shiva). Those who live by the path of Chariyai attain the state of Saalokam (residing in Shiva's world). Those who live by the path of Kiriyai attain the state of Saameepam (being near Shiva). Those who live by the path of Shivayoga attain the state of Shivasaaroopam. Such great souls who have attained Shivasaaroopam are about to attain Gnanam and Shivasaayujyam. Such great people are those who are born and live in Thiruvārūr. Therefore, they all appear as having attained Shivasaaroopam, as luminous forms, as forms of light in Shiva's light, as the light of Shiva Gnanam. By virtue of being born in Thiruvārūr, they are destined to attain Shivasaaroopam and Mukti in due course. Therefore, in accordance with the Lord's words in the dream, "All those born in Thiruvārūr are knowledgeable Brahmins; I will show them to you, and you will see them," Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, who has now arrived in Thiruvārūr, sees them all as having attained Shivasaaroopam, as luminous forms. Upon seeing this vision, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar raises his hands in worship, prostrates himself on the ground, and is filled with great joy. After this, the vision disappears. That is, the Lord, who showed everyone in Thiruvārūr as having attained Shivasaaroopam, now withdraws that vision. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar immediately requests forgiveness for his mistake and worships. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar had gone to worship the Lord of Thiruvārūr in Thirumani. There, he thought that he had become impure by touching people of various castes who had gathered together. He therefore thought he needed to bathe and purify himself. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar requests the Lord to forgive all such mistakes. Thereafter, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar leaves his village and settles in Thiruvārūr. Nayanar considered living in Thiruvārūr as a great life. In addition, he continues to perform services for the Lord and His devotees in Thiruvārūr. He performs sacred services to ensure that the daily (nithya) and occasional (naimithika) rituals, as we discussed earlier, are properly observed. He performs sacred services such as Chariyai (external worship). He also performs services for devotees. He has been performing the sacred service of providing whatever is needed for a long time to the devotees of Shiva who apply sacred ash (Thiruneeru). Due to this greatness and distinction, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman praises Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar in his Thiruppathikam as "the pure gold (aani pon) among the devotees of the Lord who dwells in the anthill (Puttidam Konda Nadhar), who resides in the beautiful anthill." Gold is inherently precious. The instrument used to measure its purity is called a touchstone (urai aani). Similarly, Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is the one who measures the devotion of great devotees. This is why Thirunavukkarasu Peruman refers to him as "Thondarkku Aani" in his Thiruppathikam. "The Lord's devoted servant, Naminandhi, who lights lamps, is like pure gold among the devotees, and his service is known throughout the world, especially during the Panguni Uthiram festival in Thiruvārūr." This verse is the second verse of the 102nd Thiruppathikam of the 4th Thirumurai, which belongs to the Thiruvārūr temple. Just like Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman also praises Naminandhi Adigal in his Thevaram:

"The Lord who is beyond comprehension, whom even Brahma and Vishnu could not find, who is worshipped by Naminandhi, the great devotee who controls his breath and meditates on the Lord with the beautiful eye, is the Lord of Kozhili, who is adorned with flowers." This is a verse by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the 6th verse of the 62nd Thiruppathikam of the 1st Thirumurai, which belongs to the Thirukkōḷili temple. Thus, the character and intense love of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar are spoken of. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman composes the concluding verse:

"In this manner, Naminandhi Adigal, whose goodness grew, performed many sacred services that all worlds worshipped, and attained a place in the beautiful radiant light that grows in the shade of the feet of the King of Thiruvārūr, who comes with the moon on his head and the river in his matted locks." Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings in the concluding verse that Naminandhi Adigal, whose goodness grew, performed many sacred services that all worlds worshipped, and attained a place in the beautiful radiant light that grows in the shade of the feet of Thyagaraja Peruman of Thiruvārūr. Nayanar worshipped Thyagaraja Peruman, the King of Thiruvārūr, as his soul's Lord. Therefore, he merged with the light of Shiva at the feet of that Lord. The shade of the feet is mentioned because it removes the heat of birth. The radiant light that grows at the feet is mentioned because it removes the darkness of ignorance, which is the root impurity (Moola Malam) of ego (Aanavam). It is customary to metaphorically refer to the feet in this way. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar by singing that Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar attained a place in the beautiful radiant light that grows in the shade of the feet of Thyagaraja Peruman. Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar is the last Nayanar in this chapter called Thirunindrachcharukkam. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar and blesses us. This chapter, Thirunindrachcharukkam, is based on the fourth verse of Thiruthondar Thogai, "Thirunindra Chemmaiye Chemmaiya Konda." According to "I am a servant to the devotees of Perunambi Kulachchirai," Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the Puranam of Kulachchirai Nayanar. According to "I am a servant to Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar," he narrates the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar and Karaikkal Ammaiyar. According to "I am a servant to Appoodhi Adigal," he narrates the Puranam of Appoodhi Adigal Nayanar. According to "I am a servant to Neelanakkar of Saththamangai, surrounded by clear water," he narrates the Puranam of Thiruneelanakka Nayanar. According to "I am a servant to Arunanambi Naminandhi Adigal," Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar, following the verses sung by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in Thiruthondar Thogai. In addition, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal concludes this fourth verse with "Arooran is a servant to the Lord of Aroor." Accordingly, Nambiandar Nambigal composes a verse in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Similarly, at the end of this chapter, Sekkizhar Peruman also composes a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. "Arooran is a servant to the Lord of Aroor" is the concluding phrase in Thiruthondar Thogai. In this fourth verse, which begins with "Thirunindra," seven devotees are mentioned. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal concludes this fourth verse by saying that he, Arooran, is a servant to these seven devotees individually and also to the Lord of Thiruvārūr. Accordingly, Nambiandar Nambigal composes a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal at this point in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. That verse is as follows:

"He who sang incomparable Tamil hymns for Nandi, our Lord, and the Lord of Thiruvārūr, and who attained the grace of His feet, which are difficult to contemplate in the flowing waters of the Kaveri, and who worships those very feet, is called Vanthondar in this world." Thus, Nambiandar Nambigal praises Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in the genre work Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. In accordance with this genre work, Sekkizhar Peruman also concludes this chapter with a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Similarly, at the end of every chapter, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes with a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. This is the structure Sekkizhar Peruman adopted. "Arooran is a servant to the Lord of Aroor" is the concluding phrase of Thiruthondar Thogai in every verse. Accordingly, at the end of each chapter of Periya Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman includes a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Therefore, the concluding verse of this chapter is the 1898th verse, which is a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in Periya Puranam. That verse is as follows:

"He who, in ancient times, on a good day, rescued a young Brahmin boy, whose chest was adorned with the five-weapon amulet, from the mouth of a lone great crocodile in the lotus pond of Thiruppukkozhiyur, those who remember his feet will be rescued from the cycle of birth and death." This is the concluding verse of Thirunindrachcharukkam in Periya Puranam, a eulogy for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Its meaning is as follows: Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, also known as Aludaiya Nambigal, rescued a young Brahmin boy, who had died on a previous day and whose chest was adorned with the five-weapon amulet, from the mouth of a lone great crocodile in the lotus pond of Thiruppukkozhiyur (Avinashi). Those who remember the feet of such a Sundaramoorthy Swamigal will be rescued from the cycle of birth and death. Sekkizhar Peruman praises Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, saying that just as Sundaramoorthy Swamigal brought back the Brahmin boy who had been swallowed by the crocodile in Avinashi temple after a long time, so too will Sundaramoorthy Swamigal rescue souls from the mouth of this birth, which clings relentlessly like a crocodile. Sekkizhar Peruman praises the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, saying that those who remember those feet will be rescued from the mouth of this birth. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the fifth chapter of Periya Puranam, Thirunindrachcharukkam, by praising the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal.
