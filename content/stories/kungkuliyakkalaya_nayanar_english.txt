Chapter 01 - Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 156, Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar Puranam. In the Thiruthondathogai (Collection of Sacred Devotees) composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, Sekkizhar Peruman has arranged and sung the Puranas (hagiographies) of the Nayanmars (devotees) in the same order as the devotees were listed. In this, the Purana of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar follows the Purana of Kannappa Nayanar in sequence. In the second verse of the Thiruthondathogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to Ilai Malindha Vel Nambi, a servant to Eripatha Nayanar, a servant to Enathinathar, and a servant to Kannappar, whose fame is abundant." In the next line, he sings, "I am a servant to Kalaiyan of Kadavur."

Kalaiyan of Kadavur refers to the Purana of Kalaiyan Nayanar, who lived in the sacred town of Thirukkadavur. Therefore, as is our custom, we shall first look at a summary of the life story of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar and then contemplate his Purana in detail through the verses of Sekkizhar Peruman. In the Chola kingdom, where the river Cauvery flows, there is a sacred town called Thirukkadavur. It is one of the eight places where the Lord performed heroic deeds, and thus it is called Kadavur Veerattam. The elders speak of eight divine plays as the heroic deeds of the Lord. The places where these eight heroic deeds occurred are called Atta Veeratta Sthalams (eight heroic places), and Thirukkadavur is one of them. Here, the heroic act of kicking Yama (the god of death) took place.

In this sacred town of Thirukkadavur, Brahmins lived with excellence. Among them was an elder named Kalaiyanar. He had great love for serving Shiva's feet and excelled in good conduct. He regularly performed the sacred service of offering incense according to the rules to Amirthakadeswarar, who resides in that place. Then, by the grace of the Lord, poverty befell him. Even after poverty struck, Kalaiyanar continued to perform the sacred service of offering incense without fail. As poverty increased more and more, he sold all his land and servants, and with the proceeds, he bought kungkuliyam (frankincense) for the Lord and continued the sacred service of offering incense without interruption.

Poverty increased further. Therefore, Kalaiyanar, his wife, his children, and his relatives suffered without food for two days, having no means of sustenance. Seeing this, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's wife took the golden thali (sacred necklace) from her mangala naan (sacred thread) and gave it to her husband, saying, "Sell this and bring rice so we can appease our hunger." Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar received the golden thali from his wife and went to sell it to buy rice. On the way, he met a merchant who was carrying a bundle of kungkuliyam. He was a merchant who sold kungkuliyam. Kalaiyanar, realizing this, thought, "If this kungkuliyam is good, if it has the fragrance suitable for the Lord, then I can buy it and offer it to the Lord. I can perform this sacred service to the Lord." He thought it was a good opportunity and decided. He asked the merchant to give him the kungkuliyam in exchange for the gold he had. The merchant, very happily, accepted the thali given by Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar and gave him all the bundles of kungkuliyam. Kalaiyanar, with joy in his heart, quickly went to Thiruveerattam, stored all the bundles of kungkuliyam in the Lord's treasury, performed the sacred service, and remained there, absorbed in thoughts of Shiva. That night, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's wife and children, suffering greatly from hunger,

fell asleep, exhausted by the hunger. Then, by the grace of the Lord, Kubera (the god of wealth) brought his wealth and filled Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's house with gold, heaps of rice, and all other riches. Lord Shiva, as the Lord, revealed this in a dream to Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's wife. That lady woke up from her dream and saw the wealth that filled her entire house. She recognized it all as the grace of the Lord and bowed down. She began to prepare sacred food for her husband. At the same time, the Lord appeared in a dream to Kalaiyanar, who was sleeping in the Lord's treasury (storage place) in the temple, and commanded, "You are very hungry. Go to your house, eat sweet milk rice, and be relieved of your suffering." Accepting this divine grace, Kalaiyanar returned to his house. He saw all the wealth in his house. He asked his wife how all this wealth had come. His wife replied that it was the grace of Lord Shiva, who is Thiruneelakandar. Kalaiyanar folded his hands in reverence and praised Lord Shiva, saying, "Is this how the Lord's grace was to also take me into His fold?" His wife arranged the dishes, seated her husband with Shiva's devotees, offered incense and lamps, worshipped, and served them this food. She performed Maheswara Puja (worship of Shiva). Thus, by the grace of the Lord, he became a wealthy person in the world.

Kalaiyanar lived serving sweet food and providing assistance to all devotees. At the same time, in a nearby sacred town called Thiruppanandal, the Lingam (iconic representation of Shiva) of the Lord was leaning. It was leaning to bestow grace upon a demoness named Thadakai. The Chola king desired to straighten it and worship it. So, he harnessed elephants and armies and tried to straighten the Lord's Lingam, but the Lord did not stand straight. The elephants and armies became exhausted and fell. The king was very worried. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar heard about this. To straighten the Lingam of Lord Shiva, he traveled from his town of Thirukkadavur to Thiruppanandal to engage in that sacred service. He was saddened to see the elephants and armies exhausted and unable to rise. He resolved, "I will share their exhaustion and try to alleviate their suffering to that extent." He took a very strong and powerful rope. He wrapped flowers around one end of the rope. This part was tied around the Lord's idol. The other end of the rope was tied around his neck. With the flower-wrapped part of the large rope around the Lingam and the other end tied around his neck like a noose, the Nayanar pulled with his neck. Thus, he tried to straighten the Lingam with the rope. He prayed to the Lord with great love that, by His grace, this idol should stand straight. Would the Lord stand by and watch this suffering come to His devotee? Immediately, yielding to the devotee's love, Lord Shiva, in the form of the Lingam, stood straight. The Devas (celestial beings) showered flowers from the sky. The elephants and armies regained their energy and rose. The Chola king prostrated at the sacred feet of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, who had straightened the leaning Lingam, and praised him. Afterwards, the king performed many sacred services for the Lord of Thiruppanandal. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar stayed there for a few days, worshipping the Lord without separation, and then returned to his Thirukkadavur. There, in Thirukkadavur,

some days later, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and Thirunavukkarasu Peruman together came to Thirukkadavur. With great joy, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar welcomed them, invited them to his home, served them food befitting their greatness, performed Maheswara Puja, worshipped them, and received the grace of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. He also received the grace of the Lord. Thus, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, having lived performing many sacred services pleasing to Lord Shiva and His devotees, attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. This is the summary of the life story of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar. Regarding the lessons we learn from the Purana of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, Sivakavimani says the following: Offering fragrant kungkuliyam incense with true devotion in the presence of Shiva is a meritorious act. Pleased by this sacred service, the Lord will bestow sweet grace like a sugarcane knot. To those who cling to His sacred feet with true devotion and a unified mind, Lord Shiva will remove the fear of death. One should cultivate melting love for Lord Shiva's feet and perform Shiva's sacred services with ever-increasing eagerness. Even when poverty and other obstacles arise during the performance of Shiva's sacred services, and suffering increases, one should not mind those sufferings or be distressed by them, but continue to perform their Shiva service without wavering. If devotees do this, Lord Shiva will enjoy that devotion and bestow incomparable supreme bliss. Even when he, his wife, children, and relatives had to go hungry for two days,

Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's act of giving the thali, which he had taken to buy rice to appease their hunger, to buy bundles of kungkuliyam for the Lord's incense service, is an excellent example that demonstrates the strength of devotion for those who love the Lord. The message we receive from the life story of this Nayanar is that Shiva's sacred services have the power to bestow worldly pleasures as well. It is the duty of a wife to ensure that her husband, children, relatives, and other family members do not go hungry and to feed them. This is the true nature of domestic life. The rare and highly esteemed act of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's wife, who gave her thali, which she always wore on her sacred thread, to obtain rice and fulfill this duty, is worthy of being celebrated as a literary work by the world of women. Thus, the abundant wealth given to that chaste lady who fulfilled her duties in domestic life, and the fact that it was revealed to her before her husband knew, and then made known to her husband through her, are gifts of God's compassion for chastity. It is proper to regard and accept wealth and other possessions received through Shiva's grace as His grace itself. Worshipping Shiva's devotees, serving them sweet food as appropriate, and performing Maheswara Puja is an excellent Shiva dharma. Lord Shiva resides within the joy of those devotees, gladly accepts the worship and food, and bestows His grace with joy.

It is the greatness of Shiva's grace that He continues to bear the divine signs of His compassionate form for the sake of a devotee, so that other beings may know and understand them. The divine signs borne on Shiva's sacred form should be worshipped as they are. Guru, Lingam, and Sangamam (spiritual teacher, iconic representation of Shiva, and the community of devotees) are the places worthy of worship. Lord Shiva will stand in these three and accept the worship, bestowing His grace. Let us continue to contemplate the sacred Purana of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar through the sacred verses of Sekkizhar. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 - Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 157, Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar Puranam. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings in the Thiruthondar Thogai, "I am a servant to Kalayan of Kadavur." In that sequence, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar in 35 verses. The Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar is a section that describes the history and character of the Nayanar named Kalayar, who performed the sacred service of offering Kungkuliyam incense to the Lord. This Puranam is the fourth in sequence in the Ilaimalindha Sarukkam. First, the Thogai Nool (summary text), then the Vagai Nool (classified text), and then the Viri Nool (detailed text) – in this order, in the Thogai Nool, Thiruthondar Thogai, Sundaramoorthy

Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to Kalayan of Kadavur." This means, "I am a servant to the devotees of Kalayanar, who was born in Thirukkadavur." Here, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal mentions the Nayanar's place and name in his song. Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, the verse about Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar is: "They say that Shiva, who tilted in the beautiful Panandal, was straightened by the rope tied around his neck, and that Kalayan of Kadavur, having given his beloved thali and obtained good Kungkuliyam, offered incense to the fire, having previously kicked Yama." This is the verse composed by Nambiandar Nambigal about Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar through the Viri Nool, Periya Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman begins to describe Thirukkadavur, the holy place where Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar was born and lived, saying that it is in the good Chola country, Ponni Nadu. Let us now contemplate the Sthala Puranam (legend of the place) and the special features of Thirukkadavur. Thirukkadavur is the 47th holy place among the Thevaram-sung places on the southern bank of the Kaveri river in the Chola country. It is one of the eight Atta Veerattam (eight heroic shrines) where Lord Shiva performed heroic deeds. This is the place where the heroic act of kicking Yama and rolling him over (Kalasamhara Veeram) for Markandeya took place. This divine play has been mentioned in many places in all the Thirumurais (collections of devotional hymns). This holy place called Thirukkadavur is currently also known as Thirukkadaiyur. It is located near Mayavaram in the fertile Chola country where the Kaveri river flows. This place has names such as Vilvavanam, Pinjilavanam, and Kadavur. This place is called Vilvavanam because once, Lord Brahma desired to receive spiritual knowledge (gnanopadesam). He worshipped the Lord. At that time, the Lord gave him a seed and commanded him, "Plant this seed in the earth. Wherever it sprouts within one muhurtham (an auspicious time unit), stay there and worship Me." Accordingly, Brahma tried planting the Vilva seed in many places. Finally, he came to this holy place of Thirukkadavur, and when he planted the Vilva seed there, it sprouted within the specified muhurtham, as the Lord had said. Lord Brahma was delighted to see this, stayed there, worshipped the Lord, and received spiritual knowledge. From then on, this place has been known as Vilvavanam, and the deity as Vilvaneswarar. This deity, with the name Aadhivilvaneswarar, is enshrined in the northern circumambulatory path of the sanctum sanctorum, in the northeast corner of a cave. Now, this place is called Pinjilavanam. Pinjilam refers to the Jasmine creeper. When Markandeya brought the Ganga river for the anointing of Amirthakadeswarar, this Pinjila (Jasmine, or Pichchi flower) creeper also came here. The flowers from this creeper are offered only to the Lord. Now, next, the name Kadavur. When the Devas (celestial beings) and Asuras (demons) churned the ocean of milk for Amrita (nectar of immortality), they collected the Amrita that emerged in a pot and brought it to this place. The Devas, led by Mahavishnu, went to bathe, having chosen this place to drink the Amrita and having placed the Amrita pot there. When they returned, they tried to take the pot containing the Amrita. They found that it had penetrated deep into the earth and had become a self-manifested Lingam, a form of Shiva. The Amrita in the pot itself was seen as a Shiva Lingam.

This is why it is called Amrita Lingam, and the Lord is named Amirthakadeswarar. This is also why this holy place is called Kadavur, Thirukkadavur, and Kadapuri. Kadavur also means the place that helps one overcome the suffering of Yama (the god of death). The Amirthakadeswarar temple in Thirukkadavur faces west and is very beautiful. Five circumambulatory paths (prakarams) are beautifully arranged, adding to its glory. There are Rajagopuram (royal temple tower) entrances on the west and east sides of the temple. Inside the temple, in the first prakaram, on the left, there is a hundred-pillared mandapam (hall) called the Prasanga Mandapam. To its east is a Nandavanam (flower garden). The gopuram on the east side is called Muniswarar Gopuram because Muniswarar resides there. Near that eastern gopuram is the temple tank (thirukkulam). In the same prakaram, in the southwest corner, there is a separate shrine for the Goddess. The presiding deity of the place is Abirami Amman, who faces east. Upon entering the second prakaram, on the left, there is an ornamental mandapam, and in the northeast direction, a Yagasalai (sacrificial hall). In the southwest, there are the Devasthanam (temple administration) office, a granary, and vehicle rooms. Upon entering the third prakaram, the Thirumaligai Pathi Mandapams (halls) are beautifully arranged. On the west side are Murugan, Lakshmi, Somaskandar. On the north side are Natarajar Sabhai, Vilvaneswarar, Bikshatanar, Bhairavar. On the east side are the Panchabhootha Lingams (five elemental Lingams) and Surya (Sun god). On the south side are Agastiyar, Santhanabhooshana Arasar (king), his minister, the merchant who came to sell Kungkuliyam, the Sapthamathargal (seven mother goddesses), and Yama, who received grace in this holy place. The 63 Nayanmar idols are also here. In the southeast corner is the Madaippalli (temple kitchen). In the Lord's shrine, there is a Sangu Mandapam (conch hall). In its southwest, there is the Sthala Vinayagar (presiding Ganesha of the place), and in the northwest, Chandrasekarar, Samayacharyar (spiritual preceptors), and Utsava Moorthams (processional deities) are enshrined. In the Mahamandapam (main hall), there is Kalasamhara Kadavul. To the north of the Lord's temple is Punniyavardhanar, to the east is Chandeeswarar, to the south is Dakshinamurthy, and the Jasmine creeper that came with Ganga is also here. Here, Kallavaranapillaiyar is the Sthala Vinayagar. We already saw, didn't we, that when the Devas, led by Mahavishnu, placed the Amrita pot here, thinking it was the right place to drink Amrita, it transformed into a Shiva Lingam? At that time, the Devas, led by Mahavishnu, prayed to Lord Shiva, asking for Amrita. Lord Shiva, bestowing His grace upon them, told them that He would provide Gnanāmṛtam (nectar of knowledge) in the Gnanavapi (sacred well of knowledge) here, and that they should drink it. When they went to search in the Gnanavapi, they did not find Amrita. So, they were saddened again. At that time, Lord Shiva appeared and told them to worship Ganesha, and they would find it. Immediately, all the Devas worshipped Lord Ganesha, who is the one with the Omkara in the heart. Vinayagar also appeared and said, "Because you forgot Me, I hid the Amrita. Therefore, you have attained the Amrita given by the Lord, yet you have not truly attained it. Cast aside your worries. As the Lord said, the Amrita will appear in the Gnanavapi."

Then all the Devas prayed to Lord Vinayagar, "May you, with the name Soraganapathi (Thief Ganesha), forever reside in this place for your devotees and grant their desired accomplishments without fail." Because he hid the Amrita pot, this Ganesha is named Kallavaranavinayagar (Ganesha who stole the pot) and Soraganapathi. The Devas thus worshipped Lord Vinayagar, consecrated Sri Kallavaranathinam on the western side of the Gnanavavi, and praised him. By the grace of Ganesha, the Amrita appeared, and the Devas drank it. Here, the names of the Lord are Amirthakadeswarar, Amudakadothbhavar, Amudakadeshar, Kalasamharar, and Kadavur Veeratteswarar. There is a crack and a scar from a noose on the Lord's idol. These remind us of the story of Markandeya. The name of the Goddess of this place is Abirami Ammaiyar. She is depicted with a rosary in her upper right hand, a red lotus in her upper left hand, the Abhaya Mudra (gesture of fearlessness) in her lower right hand, and the Varada Mudra (gesture of boon-giving) in her lower left hand. Just as Brahma worshipped Lord Shiva here and received blessings, so too did Brahmi, Saraswati Devi, worship Abirami Amman and receive blessings. There is a shrine here called Punniyavarthanar. When Pulastya came to see Markandeya, he consecrated an idol after worshipping Amirthalingesar. He is called Punniyavarthanar. Next is Papavimosanar, worshipped by Agastya. Next is Vilva Vaneswarar. This deity is the Aadi Moorthy (primordial deity), worshipped by Brahma. We have already seen his story. In this temple, Thirugnanasambandha Swamigal's Utsavar Moorthy (processional deity) is depicted in a dancing posture. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal is depicted with Paravai Nachiyar. Next, the Kalasamhara Kadavul, who bestows grace in this place, is enshrined in a beautifully sculpted hall to the north of the Mahamandapam, facing south, in the posture of having blessed Yama after kicking him. In his right hands, he holds a trident and an axe, and in his left hands, a noose and the Darshini Mudra. With his left foot, he kicked Yama, who lies fallen head down. A Shiva Bhutam (attendant of Shiva) pulls Yama, who lies fallen at the Lord's feet, with a rope. To the west of Kalasamhara Moorthy, Markandeya Muni is worshipping the Lord. To the left of Kalasamhara Moorthy, Balambikai bestows grace. Balambikai is depicted with Thirumagal (Lakshmi) and Kalaimagal (Saraswati) as her attendants on either side. The beautiful divine form of Kalasamhara Moorthy, with his slightly raised, sweet left golden foot, and the finger of his left hand pointing to that foot, indicating "this is what you should attain," and the small smile gracing his coral-red lips, and the downward-pointing trident, and Yama, who was kicked and fell at his feet, being dragged by a rope by Kundodara, and Markandeya, who was saved from Yama's noose and stands praising, and Balambikai Ammaiyar, who stands to the left, witnessing this divine play for the sake of living beings, are sweet forms that one should see and attain. In front of Kalasamhara Moorthy's shrine, Yama's Utsava Moorthy (processional deity) is present with his buffalo vehicle. Yama, who was destroyed by the Lord, was revived by the Lord's grace and received the command and boon not to approach Shiva's devotees, and he continues to praise Kalasamhara Moorthy. In this shrine, Kalasamhara Moorthy is anointed eleven times a year. He goes on a street procession only on the sixth day of the Chithirai festival. There are also idols of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's Utsavar, Murugar, Chandrasekarar, Markandeya, Rathnakadeswarar, and others. This holy place is a Thiruthalam (holy place) that has been sung by the three Thevaram (devotional hymns) composers, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and has their three Thiruppathigams (sets of ten verses). It is the holy place where Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar and Kari Nayanar were born and received grace.

Thirukkadavur is the glorious holy place where Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar and Kari Nayanar performed service to Lord Shiva and attained liberation. It is the place where Markandeya, the son of Mrikandu Muni, who thoroughly researched the truth that there is no other deity than Shiva, worshipped Amirthalingam and conquered Yama. The story of Markandeya Muni is known to all of us. Markandeya was born as the son of Mrikandu Muni. Hearing from his parents that he would live only for 16 years, Markandeya Muni performed Shiva Puja in 107 holy places. He came here to Thirukkadavur and performed Puja. This is the 108th holy place where Markandeya Muni performed Puja. While Markandeya Muni was worshipping the Lord of this place, when his lifespan was about to end, Yama, the god of death, came with his attendants on his buffalo vehicle and threw his noose, binding Markandeya along with the Lord he was worshipping. The Lord, as Kalasamhara Moorthy, emerged from the Shiva Lingam, kicked Yama, and blessed Markandeya to be a Chiranjeevi (immortal) and eternally 16 years old. We are now going to look at the Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, who was born in this place and attained liberation. We will also separately look at the Puranam of another Nayanar, Kari Nayanar. Thirukkadavur Uyyavandha Devanayanar, who authored Thirukalittrupadiyar, one of the 14 Saiva Siddhanta scriptures, was born in this place. Thirukkadavur Uyyavandha Devanayanar placed the Gnanashastra (scripture of knowledge) called Thirukalittrupadiyar, which he composed, under the Thiruvainthezhuthu Padi (five-letter step) in the Thillai Mandapam (hall) for the Thillai Vazh Andhanargal (Brahmins of Chidambaram) to accept it. The elephant form on those steps came alive and, with its trunk, took the Gnanashastra and placed it at the lotus feet of Lord Nataraja. This is why this book received the special name Thirukalittrupadiyar. The Goddess of this place, Abirami Ammaiyar, is enshrined in a separate temple facing east, in the southwest of the outer circumambulatory path next to the Amirthakadeswarar temple, with a special manifestation of divine grace. The story of Abirami Pattar, who was born in Thirukkadavur and worshipped Abirami Ammaiyar with great devotion, and the story of the great grace of the Goddess, who made a full moon appear on an Amavasya (new moon day) for Abirami Pattar, are known to all of us. Many devotees still read and benefit from the Abirami Andhadhi, which begins with "Udhikkinra Sengathir Uchchi Thilagam" (The rising red sun, a tilak on the forehead), composed by Abirami Pattar in praise of Abirami Ammaiyar of this place. Abirami Pattar's lineage, the Bharathi Vamsam, still exists today. Now, let's look at the sacred waters (theerthams) here. First, Amrita Pushkarani. This sacred tank is located between the eastern Rajagopuram and the second circuit. Those who bathe in it will be freed from the cycle of birth and death and attain a great state. On the western bank of this tank is the Amritasiddhi Vinayagar temple. Next is Kalatheertham. This is also known as Anaikkulam (elephant tank). It beautifully adorns this town. This Kalatheertham was created by Yama. On the southern bank of this theertham is the Kaleswarar temple, worshipped by Yama. Yama received a boon to bestow great grace upon those who bathe in this theertham, worship, and pray. Next is Markandeya Theertham. This is in the form of a well in the Kadavur cremation ground. At Markandeya's request, the Ganga Theertham appeared in this well. It is located in the southern part of the Thirukkadavur cremation ground, which is about one Nazhigai (a unit of time, approximately 24 minutes) away from this place. The daily anointing (thirumanjanam) of Amirthakadeswarar is performed only with the water from this well. No other water is used for the anointing of the Lord and Goddess. Furthermore, this water is not used for the anointing of any other deity except Amirthalingeswarar. This water is also carried for the Sankaabhishekam (conch anointing). On the sixth day of the Chithirai festival, on the Suklapaksha Aswini, the Lord bestows theertham here. On that day, everyone bathes in that theertham. Besides this, there are also Kaveri Theertham, Ammanaru, and many other theerthams. Next is the Sthala Viruksham (sacred tree) here, the Vilvam tree. It is considered a form of Shiva and is located in the second prakaram. The next Sthala Viruksham is the Jasmine creeper. When Markandeya brought the Ganga to anoint and worship Amirthalingam, this Pinjilam also came here. It is honored by building a platform at the Dakshinamurthy shrine, and puja is also performed for it. Those who worshipped here include Markandeya, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, Kari Nayanar, Saptha Kannigaigal (seven virgins), Navadurgaigal (nine Durgas), Sibi Chakravarthi, Brahma, Vasuki, Somasarman, Sivasarman, Yamagireedan, Rathnakaitan, Chandrabhooshanan, Brahmadhirajan, Rathnakaran, Panchasuriyargal, and many others. The surrounding temples of this place include the Kaleswarar temple on the southern bank of Anaikkulam, Pidari Kovil, Ayyanar Kovil, Kannikkoyil, and others. Ottankadu Mariamman Kovil is a famous offering shrine where a special festival is held in the month of Panguni. In this temple, there are mandapams such as the Hundred-Pillared Mandapam, the Alangara Mandapam (ornamental hall), the Ezhuthu Mandapam (writing hall), and the Sangu Mandapam (conch hall). Six daily pujas are performed in this temple according to the Karana Kamika Agamam. The Brahmotsavam (grand festival) is held in the month of Chithirai, and on the sixth day, Kalasamharam is performed. In the month of Panguni, commemorating the legend of Markandeya bringing the Kasi Ganga and anointing the Lord, Markandeya will go to Thirumeignanam and bestow theertham. Also, festivals such as Navaratri and Aadipooram are celebrated. The best among months is Karthigai. The best among weeks is Somavaram (Monday). Lord Shiva has the name Abhishekapriyar (one who loves anointing). Among all anointings, the Mahabhishekam (grand anointing) is the best. Among Mahabhishekam, Sankaabhishekam (conch anointing) is the best. Conch shells are of the right-whorled and left-whorled varieties. There are also conches such as Salanjalam and Panchajanyam. In this place, Markandeya spreads right-whorled and left-whorled conches, pours Ganga water, performs puja according to the rites, and anoints Amirthakadeswarar with the conch water. Furthermore, King Brihadeesh Maharaja performed Sankaabhishekam with devotion here to cure his worldly ailments. By drinking this Sankaabhishekam water or by witnessing the Sankaabhishekam, all diseases will be cured. These are the special features of the Karthigai Somavaram Sankaabhishekam of this place.

This is an anointing that devotees must witness. Furthermore, this is an excellent Kshetra (holy place) to perform Mrityunjaya Homam (fire ritual for conquering death), Japa (chanting), and Shanti (peace rituals). The temple of this place belongs to the Dharmapuram Aadheenam (monastery) and is well-maintained under the supervision of its heads. The place where Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, who was born in this place and attained liberation, lived is said to be in the North Madal Vasan (north inner street) of the temple complex, in the eastern corner. In this holy place, there is also a Vaishnava temple where Amrita Narayana Perumal is enshrined, located to the south of the Shiva temple's southern street. Thus, we have contemplated the glories of Thirukkadavur, the holy place where Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar was born and attained liberation, and looked at its Sthala Puranams. Now, let us continue to contemplate the Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 - Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 158, Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar Puranam. In the fertile Chola country, Thirukkadavur is a holy place where Brahmins reside. Here, Lord Shiva, with His matted locks touched by the Ganga, is enshrined. The Shiva here is the one with the red feet who kicked Yama, the messenger of death, who came at the end of the lifespan of His devotee Markandeya, and protected Markandeya. In this Thirukkadavur, red paddy fields are found in all seasons. On the bunds of the fields, there are conch shells and pearls born from them. Beside the fields, there are sacrificial halls. In all the water bodies here, red

lotus flowers are abundant. Groves of Areca nut trees are plentiful. The Brahmins (Vediyar) living in this town perform only the six duties prescribed for them. Brahmins are called Shatkarma Nirathargal (those who perform six duties). The Brahmins living in this town perform only those six duties. Because they perform six duties, Tamil texts praise Brahmins as Aru Thozhilōr (those with six professions). These are: Veda Adhyayanam (studying the Vedas), next is Adhyapanam (teaching not only the Vedas but all professions to others according to their needs). The third profession is Yajanam (performing yagnas for themselves). The fourth profession is Yājanam (performing yagnas for others). The fifth profession is Dhanam (giving alms). The sixth profession is Prathigraham (receiving alms). These six professions – Adhyayanam, Adhyapanam, Yajanam, Yājanam, Dhanam, Prathigraham – are called in Tamil "Vedam Oduthal, Odhuviththal, Vettal, Vetpiththal, Eethal, Etral." Therefore, the Brahmins living in Thirukkadavur perform only these six prescribed duties. They do not perform any other professions. Outside Thirukkadavur, there are many paddy fields. The sound of women singing Marutha Pann (a type of melody) can be heard there. Inside Thirukkadavur, in the places where Brahmins with sacred threads perform Vedic rituals, the chanting of Sama Veda can be heard. In yagnas, there are the teacher called Brahma, and three types of officiants called Hotas, Ritviks, and Udgathas. They have 16 assistant priests, four for each. Among these, the Udgathas chant the Sama Veda. Thus, their Sama Ganam (Sama Veda chanting) is always heard in Thirukkadavur. Although Sama is one of the four Vedas, when "Sama" is generally mentioned, it also refers to the Vedas in general. In the water bodies here, large buffaloes

shed milk. In the ponds where milk is shed, red carp (sengayalkal) leap, causing the water to splash. The fragrant lotuses there also smell of the buffaloes' milk. There are tall mansions that reach up to the clouds. In the sacrificial halls in those mansions, the smoke from the offerings (ahuthigal) touches the clouds above, making the clouds smell of the offerings' smoke. In such a holy place, a Brahmin named Kalayar, who was very wealthy, lived in that town. This Kalayar, praising the feet of Lord Shiva, daily possessed a mind filled with melting love and was excellent in good conduct. This Brahmin Kalayar, with the sacred thread adorning his chest, diligently performed the duties of a Brahmin and worshipped the Lord according to the Shiva Agamas (scriptures), performing Shiva's service. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the sacred thread here as "Marai Munnool" (the foremost thread of the Vedas) in Tamil. The Marai Munnool, or sacred thread, is worn according to the Vedic injunctions, chanting Vedic mantras, and it signifies the commitment to Vedic study and practice. "Munnool" or "Muppuri Nool" means a thread woven with three strands. For a Brahmachari (celibate student), it has three strands. For a householder (Ilvalvan), it has two sets of three strands, making six strands. For householders with children, it should have three sets of three strands, making a total of nine strands. Therefore, those who wear the thread in multiples of three – three, six, or nine – are praised as "Munnool Tharitta Vediyargal" (Brahmins who wear the foremost thread). Appar Swamigal also sings, "Nine strands on his chest," in the Thiru Kurunthogai, stating that the Lord's sacred thread is made of nine strands. However, the Vedas also permit kings and merchants to wear this sacred thread. Sekkizhar Peruman sings "Marai Munnool Andhanar" (Brahmin with the foremost thread of the Vedas) to indicate that Kalayanayanar belongs to the Brahmin class among the three classes that wear the sacred thread. His name is Kalayar. The name of the Lord of Thirukkadavur is Kalaiyam, Kalasam, Kudam, which refers to the Amrita Kalasam (pot of nectar) that the Devas brought here. That Amrita Kalasam itself took the form of a Shiva Lingam and became rooted in this place. Therefore, the Lord here is named Kalayar. It is customary for people living in a particular town to name themselves after the Lord enshrined there. Thus, the Nayanar born here also came to be named Kalayar. He was excellent in conduct. "Ozhukkam" (conduct) here refers to the principles of truthfulness and righteousness (Sathyam Vadha, Dharmam Sara) mentioned in the Vedas, as well as the four paths of Chariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam, and Gnanam (service, ritual, meditation, and knowledge) prescribed in the Shiva Agamas.

The Vedic path and the Saiva path – among these two, the Vedic conduct was mentioned earlier by "Marai Nool Marbin Andhanar" (Brahmin with the sacred thread of the Vedas). Furthermore, by stating that the Nayanar was devoted to Shiva, it is implied that he followed Saiva conduct. Kalayanayanar was one who offered fragrant Kungkuliyam incense, which is greatly beloved by Lord Shiva, as a sacred service. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the Kalasamhara (destruction of Yama) deed performed by the Lord in this holy place. Kalasamharam is the event where Lord Shiva bestowed grace upon the young Markandeya Muni. Lord Shiva appears to Markandeya Muni to remove his fear when he clings to the Lord. However, Yama also witnesses this divine appearance of the Lord. Just as water poured for paddy also benefits the weeds, Yama also saw the Lord's divine form. That form, which was meant to remove Markandeya's fear and bestow grace, appeared to Yama as a form that would destroy him and instill fear. These two contrasting qualities, removing fear and instilling fear, coexist with the Lord here. Nakkiradevanayanar praises this quality in his eleventh Thirumurai (collection of hymns) in the work called "Kopaprasadam," saying, "Having bestowed grace upon Markandeya who studied the Vedas, and having become a Yama to Yama who devours the lives of beings in the vast ocean world." When Yama was thus kicked by the Lord's sacred foot, he was not distressed but rejoiced. Elders say that Yama was delighted, thinking, "I have seen the sacred feet that even Brahma and Vishnu could not see." Appar Swamigal also beautifully and humorously expresses the same idea in the Thiruviruttam: "Neither Brahma, who went up, nor Vishnu, who delved down, knew; but Yama, whose mind was destroyed when he threw his noose at the boy who worshipped the Lord, knew the Lord's sacred feet, which are difficult to know." Vishnu and Brahma could not find the Lord's feet because they lacked devotion.

The Lord later appeared when they prayed to Him. The Linga Purana Thirukurunthogai beautifully describes these ways of attaining the Lord. The inner meaning of this is that one cannot see the Lord as long as ego (aanavam) exists. When ego is destroyed and one becomes selfless, the Lord appears before them. The Lord removes the suffering of time. He places one on the path of liberation, which is the ultimate bliss of Shiva. The Lord here grants both benefits: the removal of bondage and the attainment of Shiva's bliss. Removing the fear of Yama, the fear of death, is not just about the fear of Yama we face in this birth; it is about permanently removing the fear of Yama by ensuring that we are not reborn. This is the philosophy of Kalasamharam. Thayumanavar sings, "O Paraparame, I sought refuge in You, fearing the torment of the deceitful Yama and the suffering of powerful rebirth." Thayumanavar also sings, "O Paraparame, if one contemplates the suffering Yama endured for Markandeya, what fear can there be for devotees?" Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed many verses in the Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, describing this Kalasamhara Moorthy and Kalasamhara posture of the Lord in Thirukkadavur. In such a glorious holy place, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, true to his name, performed the sacred service of offering Kungkuliyam incense. Kungkuliyam is a fragrant substance that, when powdered and burned in fire, emits a pleasant aroma. It is a type of tree resin found in mountain forests. In Sanskrit, it is called Kukkulu or Kurkulu. This incense offering is prescribed in the Shiva Agamas as part of the worship of the Lord. Offering incense with Kungkuliyam is considered particularly auspicious. Lamps and incense are essential parts of worship. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's Thevaram states that the Lord bestows grace upon those who offer them with inner devotion and according to the rites. "Those who bathe in the early morning, become devotees of the Mad One, gather buds and flowers, keep love within their hearts, and are capable of offering lamps and incense according to the rites, for them, the Lord of Kadavur Veerattanam will be as sweet as a lump of sugarcane." This is the Thevaram composed by Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal, from the fourth Thirumurai, Thirukkadavur Veerattanam Thiruppathigam, verse 4. The meaning of this verse is: For devotees who bathe at dawn, become devotees of the Lord, gather buds and flowers according to custom, cultivate love in their hearts, and are capable of offering good lamps and incense according to the rites, the Lord of Thirukkadavur Veerattanam will be as sweet as a lump of sugarcane. The Shiva Dharmottaram text mentions five types of incense materials, and Kungkuliyam is one of them. "Know that Kundurugam, pure great camphor, dense dark agarwood, white sandalwood, and sweet Kukkulu are the five incense materials." This is a verse from the Shiva Dharmottaram. It states that there are five incense materials, and Kungkuliyam is one of them. Furthermore, the Agamas mention the Shodasha Upacharam (sixteen types of services) that should be performed in pujas, and Dhoopam (incense) is one of them. Dhoopam means offering fragrant smoke to the Lord as worship. This signifies the principle that our ignorance can be removed by the power of action (kriyashakti). Agarwood, frankincense, sambrani (benzoin resin), and kungkuliyam are excellent incense materials. It is believed that offering incense and worshipping the Lord removes sins. When offering incense, one should chant with the Hrudayanthram (heart mantra) and conclude with "Swaha." Furthermore, Saiva Siddhanta states that offering incense in Shiva temples and during Shiva pujas is a very meritorious act for Shiva. Incense removes impurities and bestows supreme bliss. "Dhu" refers to the removal of impurities, and "Pa" refers to joy. The Srimat Karana Agama describes the method of preparing Seethari Dhoopam. In that incense, sandalwood should be seven parts, sambrani six parts, kungkuliyam five parts, saffron four parts, cyperus rotundus (korai kizhangu) three parts, agarwood two parts, and green camphor one part. The Srimat Karana Agama states that incense should be offered in this proportion, mixed with a little cow ghee. This is mentioned in the book "Saiva Siddhanta Katturaigal" (Essays on Saiva Siddhanta). "I have never forgotten water, flowers, and incense" is also a saying from Tamil Thirumurai. Those who perform such Shiva services are truly born; others are as if unborn.

Thus, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar was performing the service of offering Kungkuliyam incense to the Lord. At that time, by the grace of the Lord, poverty befell the Nayanar. Is the suffering of poverty for devotees also the grace of the Lord? Yes, it is also the grace of the Lord. Wealth and poverty, joy and sorrow, come according to one's karma, bestowed by the Lord. "Nōvuḷār vāyvuḷāṉ" (He who has pain also has a mouth to speak) is a saying from Thevaram. Therefore, the suffering of poverty and other troubles that come to devotees are also bestowed by the Lord as tools to measure their devotion. Just as one views gold and copper equally, the Lord's grace is to bring about a state of equanimity (iruvinai oppu), where one views joy and sorrow equally, and to take devotees into His fold. Even when poverty came to the Nayanar by the grace of the Lord, he continued to perform that service without fail. Thus, as poverty came, he sold all his lands and even the servants who depended on him, and all his great wealth was depleted, yet Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar continued to perform service to the Lord. Even though the Nayanar's relatives, children, and wife, who depended on him, suffered from poverty, the Nayanar continued to perform the Lord's service without abandoning it. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 - Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 159, Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar Puranam. In the holy place of Thirukkadavur, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, who was engaged in the sacred service of offering Kungkuliyam incense to the Lord, was surrounded by poverty. Even when poverty struck, after he had spent all his lands and all the wealth he possessed, the Nayanar continued to perform the service of offering incense in the Lord's temple without abandoning it. Due to that poverty, the Nayanar's relatives and children suffered. Generally, parents endure their own suffering, no matter how great, but they cannot bear to see their children suffer. The greatest suffering of all is the suffering of one's children. Such great suffering, which no one wishes to see their children endure, is what the Nayanar experienced here due to poverty. Similarly, the Nayanar's relatives also suffered. Domestic life (Ilvazhkai) is a life lived with relatives. Relatives are an indispensable part of domestic life. "Even when one is without possessions, to appreciate old ties is the mark of relatives" (Thirukkural, Chapter on Kinship, Kural 521). This means that even when a person has lost their wealth and become poor, relatives are those who appreciate and celebrate them. They remember all the help he rendered when he was wealthy and lived gloriously, and they support him without abandoning him even when poverty strikes. This is why Thiruvalluvar dedicated a separate chapter called "Sutram Thazhal" (Cherishing Kinship). Furthermore, when a person moves from the Brahmacharya ashrama (student stage) to the Grihastha ashrama (householder stage), relatives come first, and only then do children come. That is why the author here places relatives first, saying "Sutrathodu Makkalum" (relatives and children). Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's relatives were also suffering, and his children were also suffering. By saying that his children were suffering, the suffering of the Nayanar's wife is implicitly understood. Moreover, the relatives the Nayanar had were not those who gathered when wealth came and left when poverty struck; they were relatives who always stayed together, whether in wealth or in poverty. "Like birds that leave a dried-up pond, those who depart in times of trouble are not true relatives. Like the water lilies, lotuses, and reeds that cling to the pond, those who remain united are true relatives," says Avvaiyar in her Moodurai. When a pond is full of water, water birds come and live in it, eating their food from the pond. But if the water in the pond dries up, those birds leave the pond and go to another pond that is full of water. However, the lotus, lily, and other flowers that are in that pond remain in the pond even when it is full of water, and they remain within the pond even when all its water has dried up, never leaving the pond. Similarly, good relatives are those who remain with us without ever leaving, whether in times of wealth or in times of poverty.

The relatives the Nayanar had were also those who, even when wealth came, did not leave when poverty struck, but always remained united. Therefore, the Nayanar considered it his duty to protect his relatives even in poverty, and he continued to care for them during that time of poverty. Thirukkural states that caring for relatives is one of the five duties of a householder. Thirukkural prescribes five duties for householders: "Thenpulathar, Deivam, Virundhu, Okkal, Than" (ancestors, God, guests, relatives, and oneself). Among these, "Okkal" refers to relatives. Therefore, caring for relatives, which is one of the five important duties of those engaged in domestic life, was performed by the Nayanar and his wife even when they had wealth. Even when wealth was gone and poverty came, they continued that service without interruption. Thus, when they were suffering in poverty, for two consecutive days, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's family and relatives suffered without food. Seeing his children and relatives suffering without food for two days, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's wife, unable to bear the suffering, took the gold from her thali and gave it to Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, saying, "Sell this gold and bring rice." Sekkizhar Peruman composed the verse, "When there was nothing, and food was gone for two days, seeing her children and numerous relatives, his loving wife gave the thali from her flawless sacred thread to her husband Kalayanar, saying, 'Buy rice.'" She gave the thali, which was strung on a flawless, auspicious thread, and asked him to sell it and bring rice. By doing so, she believed that her children and relatives would be able to relieve their hunger. The Nayanar's wife did this. The "Mangala Nool" (auspicious thread) is that which brings auspiciousness and is a symbol of a married woman (sumangali). During a wedding, the groom tying the mangala naan around the bride's neck is an important part of the wedding rituals. In the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter on marriage, it mentions "Mangala Naan Saathi" (tying the auspicious thread) during Meenakshi Ammai's wedding. "While the four Vedas roared and conches blared, the learned four-faced one (Brahma) performed the wedding rituals according to the scriptures, and the Lord, who grants liberation to the unattached, took the hand of the one who gave birth to the world, holding the auspicious thread." This is the 784th verse of the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter on marriage.

In the 247th verse of the Thevakanadam, in the chapter on the marriage of Theivayanai Ammai, it also mentions the event where Lord Muruga tied the mangala naan around the neck of Theivayanai Ammai during their wedding. "The Lord, who contemplated the lotus-born (Brahma) in his mind, took the mangala naan given by him and tied it around the beautiful neck of the lady, adorning her with a fragrant garland." Such a glorious mangala naan is indispensable for married women. However, when great suffering arises, it is a rule prescribed in ethical texts that one can separate the gold thali from the mangala naan and use it. Therefore, here, the Nayanar's wife, wearing only the mangala naan, separated the gold from it and gave it to the Nayanar to buy rice. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman says "Kothil Mangala Nool" (flawless auspicious thread). Therefore, it is clear to us that separating the gold from the mangala naan in this way does not diminish the auspiciousness of the mangala naan. Thus, it is understood that the mangala naan itself signifies the state of being a married woman. At that time, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar took the gold and went to buy rice and other food items. A merchant, carrying bundles of Kungkuliyam (incense resin) for sale, came towards the Nayanar. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar saw the bundles and asked, "What is in this bundle?" The merchant replied, "This is a bundle of Kungkuliyam." He also said it was for sale. Hearing this, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar was greatly pleased. He thought, "If this Kungkuliyam is good and fragrant, suitable for the worship of Lord Shiva, what greater blessing could I receive? I have become a blessed one. I must buy this Kungkuliyam, which is needed for the Lord, and add it to the Lord's temple. This is the most important service; other duties are not as important." The Nayanar felt immense joy.

The gold currently in the Nayanar's hand could help alleviate the hunger of himself, his wife, children, and relatives. But the Nayanar thought that the Kungkuliyam, which was needed for the worship of Lord Shiva, was superior to that. The unwavering devotion of the Nayanar to Lord Shiva is clearly revealed here, even though he lived a domestic life with his wife, children, and relatives. Even though he had all kinds of suffering, such as poverty, hunger, worry, and distress, he did not consider them as suffering. Instead, he thought that the joy of Shiva Puja was the only joy, and Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar rejoiced. He asked the merchant who was selling the Kungkuliyam, "Will you take this gold I have and give me the Kungkuliyam?" The merchant agreed. The Nayanar was greatly pleased. He thought, "This is a rare blessing to obtain." Thirumoolar's Thirumandiram says, "Having obtained a rare birth, those who do not worship the rare Lord, and all other rare beings, have lost a rare blessing." Offering Kungkuliyam is a great blessing and a great meritorious act for Shiva. In Thiruvaiyaru, at the southern gopuram entrance, in the Adkondaar shrine, Kungkuliyam is still continuously burned in a pit, and its smoke is offered as incense even today. Thus, the Nayanar, considering it a great blessing to buy Kungkuliyam for Shiva's service and add it to the temple, gave the gold in his hand to the merchant and received the bundles of Kungkuliyam from him. Here, Sivakavimani Ayya writes that the Nayanar was a Brahmin (Vediyar) who wore the sacred thread. He had a lot of wealth, and then all that wealth gradually disappeared, and poverty came. Even when poverty came, the Nayanar, being a Brahmin, did not engage in any other profession but continued to perform only the six duties prescribed for Brahmins.

Even when poverty came, he did not seek other professions to alleviate it, but sustained himself by selling his possessions. It was the custom of that time for people of each varna (social class) to perform only the professions prescribed for their varna and earn their livelihood through them. One might ask, "If poverty strikes, why not do some work to alleviate it? Whether good or bad, why not do some work to overcome poverty, instead of just selling one's possessions to survive? Isn't this laziness?" In those days, people, even when they faced suffering, poverty, and hunger, would not consider living by doing any profession other than their own. Such great people lived in those times. This is a truth learned from the life of our Nayanar. Our Nayanmars and elders did not despise any profession or consider any profession inferior. However, our elders and Nayanmars followed the practice of earning their livelihood only through professions appropriate to their tradition and qualification. This was because it was prescribed in ethical texts. They performed only the profession suitable for their varna. They performed only the profession suitable for their family tradition. They performed only the profession suitable for their qualification. The great Nayanmars, like Arivazhtaya Nayanar, earned their living by harvesting paddy for wages. Kaliya Nayanar earned his living by extracting oil from oil presses, which was his family profession. Similarly, many Nayanmars performed only the professions suitable for their varna, lineage, and tradition. Even when poverty struck, they did not engage in professions beyond that. But now, in this modern age, people have forgotten all these rules, forgotten the rules prescribed in ethical texts, forgotten the way of life shown by our elders, and think that all professions are the same, that anyone can do any profession, and that one can live comfortably by doing any work. Wise people will never accept this. "Even if one sees one's mother's hunger, one should not do deeds that the wise condemn," says Thirukkural, Chapter on Purity of Action, Kural 656. This means that even if one sees one's mother, who gave birth to them, suffering from hunger due to poverty, one should not engage in deeds that the wise condemn. According to this rule, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, being a Brahmin, performed only the six duties prescribed for Brahmins and did not engage in any other profession. Even when poverty struck him, he did not engage in other professions. When his elders, his wife, and his children were suffering from hunger, the general rule is that one should somehow bring wealth and alleviate their hunger. However, wise people and elders would not accept this general rule. This is evident from this. Now, the Nayanar gives the gold and buys all the bundles of Kungkuliyam and adds them to the treasury of the temple where the Lord is enshrined. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 - Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 160, Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar Puranam. The Nayanar (devotee) sells the gold from his wife's *thali* (marriage necklace), buys bundles of *kunguliyam* (frankincense), and quickly goes to the *Veerattanam* (one of the eight places where Shiva performed heroic deeds) temple of the Lord in Thirukadavur to offer them to Lord Shiva. He adds the bundles of *kunguliyam* he brought to the *Bhandaram* (storehouse) of the Lord's temple, where the Lord keeps all things as His possession and all beings as His servants. *Bhandaram* is a storage room or place in temples. It is also called *Pokkisham* (treasury). It is also customary to refer to the *Desikars* (spiritual teachers) who teach the means of liberation, which are the great divine forms of the Lord, as *Bhandara Sannidhigal*. "The Lord provides the original *Bhandaram* to His devotees; come quickly," says Thiruvachagam. After adding the bundles of *kunguliyam* to the Lord's *Bhandaram*, the Nayanar forgot all other sorrows and remained there. Forgetting all sorrows and everything else means he forgot that his wife, children, and relatives were suffering from hunger, and that he had brought the gold from his wife's *thali* to buy rice. This is the meaning. While the Nayanar remained in the *Bhandaram* of the Lord's Thiruveerattanam, forgetting himself and praising Lord Shiva, in a state of *Shiva Nishtai* (devotion to Shiva), Lord Shiva decided to bless the Nayanar's family. By Lord Shiva's divine will, Kubera caused gold and other riches, rice, and incomparable other wealth to fill the Nayanar's house everywhere. Kubera is one of the *Dikpalakas* (guardians of the directions), one of the guardians of the directions. Among the guardians of the eight directions, Kubera was appointed by Lord Shiva as the lord of the northern direction and became known as *Nidhi Kizhavan* (lord of wealth), the owner of great wealth. Kubera is also a friend of Lord Shiva. By the Lord's command, Kubera brought wealth, grains like rice, and food items to the Nayanar's house. If one asks whether it is enough to just keep gold and other treasures, or if food items and rice should also be kept, the Nayanar's family was suffering greatly from poverty. If only gold and other treasures were kept, it would take time to exchange them for food items, bring them, cook them, and then relieve their hunger. Therefore, along with other wealth, Kubera also filled the house with food items, rice, and grains. This wealth that Kubera filled the Nayanar's house with, unlike other wealth, became *Punniya Selvam* (meritorious wealth). It became meritorious wealth that came from serving Lord Shiva. Unlike other wealth, it was permanent great wealth. This wealth brought by Kubera came as a result of *Shiva Punniyam* (merit from Shiva devotion). Therefore, this same wealth later helped in the worship of the Lord, the worship of devotees, and the worship of the Saiva Samaya Paramacharyas (great spiritual teachers of Saivism) who later appeared in the Nayanar's house. The meaning of this is that things that come from *Shiva Punniyam* will again help *Shiva Punniyam*. The meaning of this is that things that arise from *Shiva Dharma* (righteousness related to Shiva) will help *Shiva Dharmas*. Thus, Kubera filled the Nayanar's house with treasures and food grains. Through this, we understand that the lady did not just respect Kalaya Nayanar as her husband, but also worshipped him as a Shiva devotee. Women perform *Pavai Nonbu* (a ritual) and sing Thiruvambavai, wishing that a devotee of the Lord should be their husband. We should apply this here to this lady. That is why the lady not only cooked food for her husband but also offered sacred food to all Shiva devotees with the wealth that came. Furthermore, it is a rule that no devotee should eat alone, but should eat with Shiva devotees. We saw how *Maheswara Puja* should be performed according to the *Shivagamas* (Shaiva scriptures) in the Puranam of Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar. Devotees who are worshipped and fed according to these rules are *Maheswaras*. Performing *Maheswara Puja* is feeding sacred food to Shiva devotees. *Maheswaras* are of four types: *Desikar* (Acharya), those who have received *Nirvana Diksha* (initiation into liberation), *Vishesha Diksha* (special initiation), and *Samaya Diksha* (initiation into the path). Feeding them sacred food after worshipping them according to custom is called *Maheswara Puja*. Those who worship Maheswara are *Maheswaras*. Those who have not received *Shiva Diksha* (initiation into Shiva) are not qualified to be *Maheswaras*. Those who condemn Shiva, the Guru, the religion, and the Shaiva scriptures, those who have stolen Shiva's property, and those who have abandoned their daily rituals are also not qualified for *Maheswara Puja*. Those who worship *Maheswaras* and feed them food will attain *Shiva Padam* (the state of Shiva). Thus, feeding *Maheswaras* who are *Samaya Diksha*, *Vishesha Diksha*, *Nirvana Diksha*, and Acharyas is called *Maheswara Puja*. Feeding others who are not these is called *Annadanam* (charitable feeding). That *Annadanam*, feeding others, is *Pashu Punniyam* (merit related to the individual soul). Therefore, it leads to good enjoyments like heaven. But *Maheswara Puja* performed for *Maheswaras* becomes *Shiva Punniyam* and helps one attain *Shiva Padam*. Those who perform *Maheswara Puja* by having those who have received the four types of *Diksha* – *Samaya Diksha*, *Vishesha Diksha*, *Nirvana Diksha*, and *Acharya Abhishekam* (consecration as an Acharya) – will attain the four types of *Shiva Padam*: *Shiva Salokyam* (residing in Shiva's world), *Shiva Sameepyam* (being near Shiva), *Shiva Sarupyam* (having Shiva's form), and *Shiva Sayujyam* (union with Shiva). Similarly, in countless sacred places, when the Lord manifested His divine grace, we can still see Him enshrined with those very signs. In Perur, near Coimbatore in Tamil Nadu, there are hoof marks of a calf of Kamadhenu (divine cow) on the Lord's *Shivalingam*. In Thirumullaivayil, the cut mark from a sword is on the Lord's *Lingam*. In Kanchipuram, when the Goddess was worshipping a *Shivalingam* and the Lord caused the Kampa River to flood, the Mother embraced the *Shivalingam*. At that time, the marks of the Mother's bangles and breasts were imprinted on the *Shivalingam*. Even today, the Lord appears in Kanchipuram with those marks of the Mother's bangles and breasts. Thus, in many places, the Lord still appears with the signs of His divine grace. Similarly, here in Thiruppanandal, for the woman named Thadagai, He stood in a leaning form, and even now, He appears as a leaning *Lingam*. This was the case until the time of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar; the Lord remained there as a leaning *Lingam*. The king of that country, seeing the Lord's *Shivalingam* leaning, was saddened and greatly desired to straighten it and worship it. Generally, the Lord remains in those sacred places with those signs so that everyone can witness His divine grace. This is because devotees who come later should see those signs on the *Shivalingam*, remember the Lord's divine plays and gracious deeds, and thereby their devotion to the Lord should grow. The Lord appears in those forms to bestow grace upon beings. It is an *Agamic* (scriptural) rule that these signs should be seen and worshipped as they are. Here too, the king should have worshipped the leaning *Lingam* as he saw it. It is wrong to think of repairing the *Lingam* as if it were a damaged temple or other structure. That is, if a part of a temple is damaged, it is repaired. But it is wrong to think of repairing the leaning *Lingam* in the same way. Even today, some people, transgressing the rules, displace, elevate, transform, and repair the Lord's idols in various ways as they wish. This is a great mistake. According to *Agamic* rules, all temples should be worshipped as the Lord's form appears. We should not elevate, lower, repair, or transform the Lord's *Lingam* or other idols as we wish. This is a great mistake. Even in temples, only damaged parts should be repaired, and they should be restored to their original state before the damage occurred. This is the rule. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar's Thirumandiram, in the second *Thanthiram* (chapter), 19th section, contains verses under the title "Thirukkoil Kutram" (Faults in Temples). It condemns faults that occur in temples. The first verse states: "If a fixed *Lingam* is removed and installed elsewhere, before it is done, the king's rule will be destroyed. Before he dies, a great disease will afflict him. Our leader Nandi has declared this." It means that if an immovable *Shivalingam* in a temple is removed and installed in another temple or another place, before that act is completed, the king's rule will collapse. The one who committed this crime will suffer from leprosy before his death and die in great pain. Thirumoolar says that our leader Nandi Peruman has firmly declared this to us. But here in Thiruppanandal, the Lord remains as a leaning *Lingam* so that everyone can realize His divine grace. The king, out of love, desired to straighten that leaning *Lingam* and worship it. Although the king undertook this action out of love and devotion to the Lord, we should keep in mind that this is also love that transgresses the rules. But the king, having great devotion and enthusiasm for Lord Shiva, tried to straighten the Lord's *Lingam*. He used his elephant armies and other armies, commanding them to try and straighten the *Shivalingam*. He made great efforts. Although the king acted out of love and enthusiasm, the Lord remained without straightening His form. This is the implication here. The king tried to straighten the *Shivalingam* using the strongest elephants in his elephant armies. Here, the king greatly valued the strength of his elephant armies, but he did not consider the strength of the Lord who gave them strength. He did not attempt it with a humble mind, considering the Lord's immense strength and power. Therefore, the Lord did not stand straight. His efforts did not yield results. But in the Nayanar's story, when the Nayanar tied a rope around his neck, the Lord stood straight. The Lord, who was unattainable to Brahma and Vishnu, who searched for His feet and head with their body and soul, later appeared and blessed them when they realized their powerlessness and praised Him. Similarly, the king tried to straighten the Lord with his strength, the strength of his armies, and the strength of his elephant armies. But the Lord did not allow it. But when the Nayanar, risking his life for the Lord, tied a rope around his neck and tried to straighten the Lord, the Lord straightened Himself. The Lord yielded to the Nayanar's boundless love and stood straight. This is the story we are about to see. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar heard about the king's attempts to straighten the leaning *Lingam*. He understood that the king was doing this out of great love and desire for the Lord. Hearing this, the Nayanar set out from Thirukadavur to worship the Lord enshrined in Thiruppanandal. On the way, he visited all the other sacred places and reached Thiruppanandal. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, who set out from Thirukadavur, first visited the Lord of Thirukadavur, then came to the sacred place of Thiruakkur and visited Thanthondrinathar, then visited Suvarnapureeswarar in Semponnar Koil, then came to Mayavaram, Thirumayiladuthurai, and visited Mayuranathar, then came to the sacred place of Thiruthuruthi and visited Uttharavedheeswarar, then came to Thiruvavaduthurai and visited Masilamani Eeswarar, then came to Aduthurai and worshipped the Lord, then came to Thirumangalakudi and worshipped Pranavaratheeswarar. Thus, he visited all the sacred places on the way from Thirukadavur to Thiruppanandal and reached Thiruppanandal. It is a rule of pilgrimage that those who travel from one sacred place to another should visit all the places on the way before reaching and visiting the intended destination. We read in the Puranam of Thaduthadkonda that when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal worshipped in Thiruthuraiyur, he desired to worship Nataraja Peruman in Chidambaram. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who intended to go to Chidambaram, visited Thiruvadhigai Veeram, Thirumanikkuzhi, Theerthanagiri, and other places on the way. Similarly, here, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar set out from Thirukadavur, visited all the sacred places on the way, and reached Thiruppanandal. Those who attempt to travel from one sacred place to another should worship the places on the way. Even if the sacred places on the way have been worshipped before, they should be worshipped again. This rule is stated in Periya Puranam. It is a rule shown by our *Samayacharyas*. Therefore, the practice of going directly from one place to another and visiting only that place, which we follow now, is improper. This is what we learn from this. Thus, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar reached Thiruppanandal. He reached Thiruppanandal, a land where Vedic scholars live, who nurture and maintain the three fires so that the Vedic path does not weaken. He saw the elephants and armies, engaged in the service of straightening the *Shivalingam* in that temple, exhausted and suffering from their hard work. Therefore, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar thought that he too should share in their suffering. Here, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar did not try, saying, "I will also join them and make the *Shivalingam* stand straight." He did not try, saying, "Like the elephants and armies, I will also use my strength to make the Lord stand straight." Because it would be foolish to try to do something that powerful elephants and soldiers could not do with his own strength. It is not the act of a wise person. If he were to think, "I will make the *Shivalingam* stand straight," it would be an attempt born of arrogance. Moreover, as we have already seen, it would be contrary to the *Shiva Dharma*. Therefore, here, the Nayanar thought that this was impossible for him. But now, what the Nayanar thought was, "Let us share in the suffering and exhaustion of those elephant armies, and try to reduce their suffering." This was the Nayanar's thought here. The Nayanar thought that he too should share in the suffering of the elephants and armies. The Nayanar is now trying only with the thought that if he shares even a little in their suffering, their suffering will be reduced a little. He came to Thiruppanandal to see the king, having heard that the king was making such efforts out of love for the Lord. Reducing the suffering of the elephants and armies who are engaged in this loving service of the king and suffering there is also the duty and righteousness of devotees. It is also the duty and righteousness of Shiva devotees. Therefore, the Nayanar tried. Even in worldly life, we see that when people who share the same love share in each other's suffering, that suffering is reduced, and when they share their joy with others, that joy increases. Similarly, here, the Nayanar began to try with the thought that if he shared in the suffering of those creatures, elephants, and soldiers who were engaged in the Lord's service and were exhausted and suffering, their suffering would be reduced a little. The Nayanar took a strong rope. He threaded flowers on one end of it. He made it into a loop and placed it on the *Shivalingam*. Because the rope the Nayanar took was very strong, if he placed it directly on the *Shivalingam*, the rope would hit the *Shivalingam* and cause harm to the Lord. Therefore, to prevent harm to the Lord, he wrapped the rope with flower garlands and silk cloths, and then placed it on the *Shivalingam*. But he made a loop on the other strong end of the rope and put it around his neck. Now, one part wrapped with flowers was on the Lord's *Shivalingam*, and the other part of the strong rope with knots was around the Nayanar's neck. Now, the Nayanar worshipped and praised the Lord, and began to pull the rope that was around his neck. In accordance with the saying, "Meditate with a unified mind," the Nayanar's mind was unified with the Lord, and the rope was like a rope twisted with threads of love. "Plant the staff of relationship and churn with the rope of awareness, and He will stand before you," says Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal's Thevaram. The Nayanar tied the rope made of flowers around the Lord and put the other end of that strong rope around his neck and suffered. In reality, what the Nayanar tied and pulled was a bond of love. It was a rope. As the Nayanar tried to pull the Lord's form with his neck, the Lord, who yielded to the Nayanar's love, would He remain leaning any longer? He would not. The Lord stood straight. The *Shivalingam* stood straight. The Lord stood straight not by the strength of the strong rope that the Nayanar wore externally, but by yielding to the rope of love that he tied within his heart. This is the meaning. If the Lord stood straight by the strength of that strong rope, He should have stood straight when the elephants and soldiers pulled Him before. But the Lord, who did not stand straight then, now yielded to the Nayanar's rope of love, when the Nayanar pulled with a unified mind, and stood straight. This love of the Nayanar is firm love. "Those who desire something will attain it if they are firm in their desire," says Thirukkural. His wife, children, and relatives suffered without food for two days, and he used the *thali* that was meant to buy rice for them to buy *kunguliyam* for the Lord. The firmness of that love is now evident. Seeing the Lord thus stand straight, the Devas rejoiced above. They cheered from the sky and showered flowers. At that time, the armies and soldiers, whose exhaustion the Nayanar tried to share, also rose with great joy. The king folded his hands, worshipped the Nayanar, and bowed at his golden lotus feet, placing them on his head. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 - Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 163, Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar Puranam. In Thiruppanandal, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar straightened the leaning *Lingam* of the Lord. Seeing this, the Devas in the sky rejoiced and showered *Karpaga* (wish-fulfilling) flowers. The elephants and armies, who had been exhausted and weakened by trying to straighten the *Shivalingam*, were greatly delighted. Like a withered grove that suddenly flourishes upon receiving rain after a long drought, they rejoiced. The elephants and armies, who had been weakened without straightening the Lord's *Lingam*, now had the opportunity to see the Lord standing straight, which they had longed for. Therefore, they rejoiced. The king praised the Nayanar and fell at his lotus feet, worshipping him. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar, a devotee, straightened the leaning *Shivalingam*. Sekkizhar Peruman states that straightening the *Shivalingam* of the Lord, who bent the Meru mountain as a bow in the chariot pulled by the Vedas, to burn the three flying cities, can only be achieved by devotees who possess a virtuous love for the Lord's feet. Who can show or witness such special love of devotees? Here, the *Thripura Dhahanam* (burning of the three cities) mentioned by Sekkizhar Peruman has been extensively discussed in the Puranam of Viranmin Nayanar. We have seen how the Lord ascended the Vedic chariot and burned the three cities. We should remember all those events here. Why does Sekkizhar Peruman mention this divine play here? Because Lord Shiva bent the Meru mountain as a bow. Meru mountain is the greatest of all mountains, the king of mountains. Lord Shiva bent such a strong Meru mountain as a bow. That Lord Shiva stood leaning in Thiruppanandal. The *Shivalingam* of Lord Shiva, who bent the mighty Meru as a bow, was leaning. It was the devotee Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar who made that leaning *Shivalingam* stand straight. The mighty Lord, who made the Meru mountain bow, was leaning here. And it was our Nayanar who straightened that leaning Lord. Does this not reveal the Nayanar's love for the Lord's feet? The love that such a Nayanar and other devotees had for the Lord's feet is immeasurable. The Lord's feet, which were unattainable to Thirumal (Vishnu) even after searching to the netherworld, are attained by the love of devotees. Thiruvachagam's *Potri Thiru Agaval* describes this as follows: "The lotus feet, which Brahma and other Devas, led by the four-faced one, worshipped and cried out, and which Thirumal, who pressed down the three worlds with two feet, and the sages of the four directions, with their five senses blossoming, praised, could not be known by the great Thirumal, who became a fierce boar and dug through the seven worlds, and then, exhausted, praised the primal Lord, saying 'Jaya Jaya'." Thus, Lord Shiva was unattainable to the Devas, Thirumal, and Brahma who searched for Him. But He was easily accessible to His devotees. The reason for His accessibility was the unified love that His devotees had for Him. It was the quality of servitude born of that unified love. All inanimate objects here are the Lord's possessions. All other living beings are the Lord's servants. Therefore, the Lord will be accessible to devotees who serve Him, realizing that quality of servitude. All worshipped the *Shivalingam* that now stood straight. The king built a temple there and performed many services in Thiruppanandal. Because the king should have made many entrances in the temple, mandapams, and walls to accommodate the elephants and armies. He should have repaired them. The temple itself should have been repaired to the extent that elephants and armies could pull it together. Now the Lord stood straight. Therefore, the temple had to be restored to its former state. The king did so and repaired the temple. He repaired the sacred garden, sacred pond, and sacred walls. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar stayed there until the king completed all the services. The king also stayed there and personally supervised and carried out the services of the Shiva temple. It is a rule that devotees who undertake services for Shiva temples should not entrust the work to others and attend to other matters, but should personally supervise the work until it is completed. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman says that the king himself stayed and completed all the services. Thus, after performing all the services and worshipping the *Shivalingam*, the king returned to his capital. Our Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar also praised the Lord's lotus feet, which perform the dance in the hall, and after a few days, returned to Thirukadavur. He continued his services as before in Thirukadavur. During those days, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the incomparable leader of Sirkazhi, and Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, the renowned king of *Thandagam* (a type of poetic meter), came together to Thirukadavur. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Thirugnanasambandha Peruman here as *Nigaril Kazhi Thalaivar* (incomparable leader of Kazhi). This can be interpreted in two ways: as an incomparable leader, and as a leader who incarnated in the incomparable sacred place of Sirkazhi. The incomparable greatness of Sirkazhi is seen in the Thiruppathigams composed by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman for Sirkazhi. The Thiruppathigams composed by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman for Sirkazhi, such as Thiruviragu, Pal Peyar Pathu, Thiru Ezhukutrirukkai, Thiru Chakkaramattu Eeradi, Thiruviyamagam, etc. Furthermore, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praises the incomparable greatness of Sirkazhi in his Thevaram as "Peruneriy Piramapuram" (Brahmapuram of the great path), "Narpulavar Tham Pugazh Porpathi" (golden city praised by good poets), "Bodhanai Pon Maraiyor Payilum Pugali" (Pugali where learned Brahmins practice), "Kalaiyin Mevum Manathor Irapporkku Karappilar Poliyum Andhan Pozhil Soozhndha Azhagarum Pugazhi" (beautiful Pugazhi surrounded by groves where those with artistic minds and generous hearts shine), "Poyya Navil Andhanar Vazhum Puravam" (Puravam where truthful Brahmins live), "Kari Thannai Panthori Seithon Pavani Seyyum Pathi" (the city where the elephant-taming one performs His procession), "Kadhalal Saivar Pasupathargal Vanangum Shanbai Nagar" (Shanbai Nagar worshipped by Saivas and Pasupathas with love), "Uravar Kavithai Pulavarkku Oru Nalum Karava Van Kai Kattrvar Serum Kalikkazhi" (Kalikkazhi where learned poets with generous hands gather and never hide their poems), "Kattral Kettal Udaiyargal Vazh Kalikazhi" (Kalikazhi where learned and wise people live), "Vilangal Amar Puyal Marandhu Meen Sanipu Kudhunsalikkum Kalanthanum Kalangalila Peruvan Kaiyudaiya Meyyar Vazh Kazhumalame" (Kazhumalam where true devotees with great generosity live, even when the clouds forget to rain and the fish suffer from drought), and "Thirunthadakkai Muthazhalor Manaigal Thoram Iraivanadhu Thanmai Padi Karunthadang Kannargalal Panthu Ammanai Pattayarum Kazhumalame" (Kazhumalam where dark-eyed women play ball and sing Ammanai songs, praising the Lord's nature in every house of the three-fired Brahmins), and "Parakkum Van Pugazhar Pazhiyavai Parththu Pala Pala Arangalai Payitri Karakkumaru Ariya Vanmaiyal Vazhum Kazhumala Nagar" (Kazhumala Nagar where those with widespread fame, seeing faults, practice various virtues and live with unknown generosity), and "Kalaiyarodu Sruthi Thogai Kattror Migu Kootam Vilaiyayina Settor Tharu Vezhunupuram" (Vezhunupuram where learned scholars and those who have mastered the Vedas gather, and where those who have overcome evil reside). Thirugnanasambandha Peruman has sung about the incomparable greatness of Sirkazhi in his Thevaram hymns. Now, if we take "Nigaril" as an epithet for Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, meaning "incomparable leader," then the incomparable leadership of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman becomes evident to us here. We will discuss this in detail in the verses of the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha. His leadership is incomparable due to many events in his Puranam, and due to the greatness of being worshipped and praised by Paramacharyas like Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Aludaiya Nambigal (Sundaramoorthy Swamigal), and religious founders like Shankaracharya. Furthermore, we can see the incomparable leadership of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman through the Lord's gracious act of bestowing liberation upon those who reach Him, and through many other deeds. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman as *Thandaga Chathurar* (master of Thandagam) here. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman is uniquely skilled in *Thiruthandagam*. *Thandagam* is a poetic meter structured with a majority of twenty-six letters, excluding *otru* (consonant dot), forming a type of Tamil musical composition with a specific rhythm. It has two divisions: *Alavazhi Thandagam* and *Alaviyatra Thandagam*. Among the three principal composers of Thevaram, only Thirunavukkarasu Peruman composed Thevaram hymns in this *Thandagam* meter. These *Thiruthandagam* hymns, when recited, melt the flesh and soul of the reciters and excel in inspiring devotion to the Lord. Due to this, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman is given the special title *Thandaga Vendhar* (King of Thandagam). *Chathurar* means one who is skilled or powerful. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman praises Thirunavukkarasu Peruman as *Thandaga Chathurar*. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's fame as *Alar Pugazh* (widespread fame). We will see his fame and his ability to enslave people later in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, the Puranam of Apputhi Nayanar, and in many other places. Sambandha Peruman and Thirunavukkarasu Peruman came together to Thirukadavur. We will see the story of these two great souls worshipping the golden feet that kicked Death, and sitting in Kalayanar's sacred monastery and being worshipped by Kalayanar, in the 247th verse of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's Puranam and in verses 533 to 536 of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Puranam. The second meeting of these two great Paramacharyas took place in Thiruppugalur. From Thiruppugalur, these two traveled together, worshipped the sacred place of Thiruambar, and on their way, came to Thirukadavur and visited the Lord of Thirukadavur. After this, they worshipped in Thiruakkur, Thiru Meeyachur, Thiru Pambaram, and other places, and reached Thiruveezhimizhalai. This is their story. We will discuss all these in detail in their respective Puranams. Thus, as the two Paramacharyas came to Thirukadavur, Kalaya Nayanar, a devotee who had boundless love for them, offered them sacred food in his sacred monastery. When he saw these two great Paramacharyas and great devotees together, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar was filled with unprecedented joy, which surged higher and higher. He was filled with boundless joy, thinking that this was the result and reward of his service of feeding Shiva devotees until now. Because Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who were filled with immeasurable and boundless love, were enslaved by the Lord due to the abundance of their love. They became Paramacharyas who ruled the world. Therefore, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar rejoiced with great love for them. Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar offered sweet sacred food to devotees and performed *Jangama Puja* (worship of living devotees), and as a result, he also received the rare service of these two great Paramacharyas, worshipped them, and received the Guru's grace. Through this Guru worship, he also received Shiva's grace, which is obtained through *Lingam* worship. Thus, Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar received the blessings of all three types of worship: Guru worship, *Lingam* worship, and *Jangama* worship. Therefore, it is understood that through Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar's *Maheswara Puja*, which was feeding devotees, he received Lord Shiva's grace and the Guru's grace. "If one gives something to the Lord in a temple with a picture, it is for the Lord in the moving temple. If one gives something to the Lord in the moving temple, it is for the Lord in the temple with a picture," says Thirumoola Deva Nayanar's Thirumandiram, isn't it? Thus, when *Maheswara Puja* is performed, it is true that Shiva, the Lord, delights within those devotees and partakes of it. Just as grass and other foods fed to a cow turn into blood, spread throughout its body, and then transform into milk that emerges from its udder, the food offered to devotees transforms into the Lord's grace and manifests in the *Shivalingam*. This is the conclusion of the *Gnana Shastras* (scriptures of wisdom). Therefore, the *Maheswara Puja*, which is feeding devotees, that *Jangama* worship, became the cause of Guru worship and *Lingam* worship, and through it, all three – *Jangama* worship, Guru worship, and *Lingam* worship – were established, and the grace of all three was received. This is what we learn from this. Thus, our Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar lived in Thirukadavur, where Lord Shiva, who punished Manmatha (god of love) with a sugarcane bow and Yama (god of death), is enshrined. As his love grew more and more, and his desire increased, a state of unified mind arose, and he performed many services that came his way. Then he attained *Shiva Padam*, the shade of the Lord's feet. The service performed by Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar was feeding devotees and performing Shiva's services. Kalayanar regularly performed the Shiva service of offering *kunguliyam* incense and the *Maheswara Puja* of offering sweet food to devotees. As his love and desire grew, he performed many other services excellently. Through these services, he attained *Shiva Padam*, the fullness of Shiva's grace. That is, he joined the pervasive power of Shiva's grace. He was freed from the heat of birth and attained the cool shade of the Lord's feet. He attained *Shiva Padam*. That *Shiva Padam* is the *Shiva Sayujya Mukthi* (union with Shiva), the great path from which one does not return to birth. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman says, "Having bowed to Kalaya Nayanar, who possessed the quality and firmness of offering his wife's long *thali* to the Lord and ensuring that the service of *kunguliyam* incense was never interrupted, I will now begin to praise the sacred service of Manakkanjara Nayanar with the help of his grace." Sekkizhar thus concludes the Puranam of Kungkuliyakkalaya Nayanar and begins the Puranam of Manakkanjara Nayanar. We will continue to contemplate accordingly.
