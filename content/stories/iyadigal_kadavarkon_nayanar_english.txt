Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 01 — Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 978, Poiyadimaiyilladha Pulavar Sarukkam, Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar Puranam, composed by divine Sekkizhar Peruman. In the Periya Puranam, following the sacred Puranam of Sakthi Nayanar, is the Puranam of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar. This Puranam is the eighth in the eighth Sarukkam (chapter), the Poiyadimaiyilladha Pulavar Sarukkam. First, in the Thogai Nool (summary text), our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to the devotees of Iyadigal Kadavarkon." In the seventh verse, this means, "I am a servant to the devotees of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar." Now, in the Vagai Nool (detailed text), the 56th verse of Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, praises Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar.

That verse is as follows: "Sakthi thadakkai kumaran natradhai than thanamellam muthippadham oror venba mozhindhu mudiyarasaamathirkku mummai nanthal aratkai iyyam etral ennum paththik kadal Iyadigal aagindra nam Pallavane." This means that this Pallava king, named Iyadigal, who is like an ocean of devotion, went to all the holy places of Lord Shiva, the father of Lord Kumara, who wields the powerful spear of divine energy. He composed one Venba (a type of Tamil verse) for each place, aiming for liberation, and praised them. Although he was a crowned king, he considered begging for alms for Lord Shiva to be far superior to his royal status, displaying immense devotion. This is the meaning from the Vagai Nool. Now, in the Viri Nool (expanded text), the Periya Puranam, the 47th Puranam is that of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Puranam of this Nayanar in 8 verses. The first verse is: "Vaiyanigal Pallavar tham kulamarabin vazhi thondri veyya kaliyum pagaiyum migai oliyum vagai adakki seyya sadaiyavar Saiva thiruneriyal arasalippar Iyadigal neethiyal adippaduthum sengolaar." The Pallava king's lineage is one of the royal clans that descended from the Cholas. The Pallavas' emblem is the bull. Our Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar was born into the lineage of the Pallavas, who ruled this world with great renown. He was a king who eradicated both internal and external enemies. He ruled in such a way as to remove severe poverty, enmity, and the suffering caused by enemies. He ruled by following the sacred Saiva path of the Lord with matted red hair. King Iyadigal Kadavarkon ruled with a just scepter, bringing the entire world under his sway through his righteous rule. In this verse, the Nayanar's sacred name is stated as Iyadigal. Pallava kings mostly adopted Sanskrit names, as was their tradition. Therefore, we do not know the birth name of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar. Nambiandar Nambigal praises him as "Bhakthi Kadal Iyadigal" (Iyadigal, the ocean of devotion), which suggests that he adopted the name Iyadigal due to his abundant devotion from a young age. "Ai" means divine, so Iyadigal means one who is a servant to the divine Lord Shiva. "Kadavar" refers to the Pallava royal lineage. Kanchipuram was the capital city from which he ruled. The second verse is: "Thirumaliyum pugazh vilanga seyal nilathil evvuyirum perumaiyudan inidhu amara pira pulangal adippaduthu tharuma neri thazhaithonga tharani mel Saivamudan arumaraiyin thurai vilanga arasalikkum annaalil." He ruled so that wealth flourished, fame shone brightly, and all living beings in this vast world lived happily and with dignity. The mention of "all living beings" indicates that not only humans but also all other creatures received the protection of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar and lived happily without want. He removed the troubles that came to his country from enemies, subdued them, and brought them under his control, ruling with justice. He ruled so that the path of Dharma flourished, and both the Saiva path and the Vedic path shone brightly in this world. The third verse is: "Mannavarum pani seyya vadanoool then Tamil mudalaam pannukalai pani seyya paaralippar arasaatchi innal ena nayandhu alippar aayinaar Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar." All other kings served him according to his commands. He excelled in arts, making all the arts, including Sanskrit (the northern language) and Tamil (the southern language), subject to him. Even crowned kings being skilled in arts is a special quality of Tamil kings. Despite all this, Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar thought that worldly rule was a source of suffering and thus rejected it. Protecting Dharma, ensuring that all beings under his rule were free from the five types of fears, was a rare and difficult task. Moreover, all these things, instead of leading to liberation for the soul, would only lead to repeated births. Therefore, he decided that this rule was a source of trouble and resolved to renounce it. He therefore crowned his son and entrusted the rule to him. Was it appropriate for him to bestow upon his son the rule that he himself had scorned? He did so out of a sense of duty, recognizing that someone was needed for the protection of this world. Renouncing his rule and entrusting it to his son, our Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar devoted himself to performing sacred service, desiring to follow the good Saiva path. The fourth verse is: "Thondu urimai purakkindraar ulagin kan andar piraan amarndharulum aalayangalanaaiyellam kandu irainji thiruthondin kadanetra pani seythe vandhamizhin mozhi venba orondra vazhuthuvaar." The Nayanar diligently performs acts of sacred service. He visits and worships all the temples where Lord Shiva, the Lord of the celestials, resides in this world surrounded by oceans. He journeys to and worships all the holy places of Lord Shiva, the Lord of Lords. The temples where Lord Shiva resides are in this earthly world. Therefore, the Thiruvachagam states that we have the opportunity to worship Lord Shiva and attain blessings by visiting the temples in this earthly world. Thus, he journeys to all holy places, performs all the duties of sacred service, such as Chariyai (ritualistic service), and for each holy place, he composes a Venba, praising the Lord. He praises the Lord by composing a Venba in rich Tamil. The next verse explains that he began this pilgrimage from Thillai (Chidambaram). The fifth verse is: "Perundhezhu kaadhalil vanangi perum patra than puliyoor Thiruchitrambalaththu aadal purindharulum seyya sadai niruthanaar thirukkooththu nerndhu irainji nedundhagayaar virippinudan senthamizhin venba menmalar punaindhaar." With overflowing love, he worshipped at Perum Patra Puliyoor (Chidambaram). He directly worshipped the sacred dance of the Lord with red matted hair, who performs the sacred dance in Thiruchitrambalam. With great devotion, he offered a soft flower, which is a Venba in classical Tamil. This is a "sol malar" (flower of words). It is a "paa maalai" (garland of verses), composed of four types of words, like a garland made of four types of flowers. Thus, starting from Thillai, Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar visited each holy place, composing one Venba for each. These Venbas were compiled as "Kshetra Thiruvendha" and included in the 11th Thirumurai by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. The first holy place Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar visited was Thillai. Therefore, the first verse in Kshetra Thiruvendha is dedicated to Thillai Chitrambalam: "Odungindra neermai olithalume utraarum kodugindraar mooppum kurugitru naadugindra nalla Chitrambalame nanna munne Thillai Chitrambalame ser." The sixth verse is: "Avvagaiyaal arul petru angu amarndhu sila naal vaiki ivvulagil tham perumaan koilgal ellam eythi sevviya anpodu panindhu thiruppani etrana seythe evvulagum pugazhndheththum in Tamil venba mozhindhaar." In that manner, having received grace in Thillai, he stayed there for a few days, performing sacred service. Afterwards, he visited all the temples where his Lord resides in this world. With pure devotion, he worshipped and performed all the sacred services. He then composed sweet Tamil Venbas, which are praised by all worlds. 24 Venbas composed by Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar have been found. These are compiled as "Kshetra Thiruvendha" in the 11th Thirumurai by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. These Venbas were composed as he visited and worshipped at each holy place. Therefore, this "Kshetra Thiruvendha" is not a book composed at one time in one place. We currently have 24 Venbas, all of which are Nerisai Venbas (a type of Venba). The verses in this collection mostly speak of the impermanence of the body and the impermanence of wealth. This body will age and fall in a few days; death will come. Royal wealth and other possessions are not permanent. Therefore, O people of the world, worship Lord Shiva immediately and attain salvation. The way to worship Lord Shiva and reach Him is to visit and worship His holy places where He resides, manifesting out of His compassion for these souls. This is the good advice Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar gives to the people of this world. Although Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar was a crowned king, he renounced his rule upon realizing impermanence and performed service, worshipping at all the holy places of Lord Shiva. The book he composed serves as internal evidence for this history. Therefore, it is understood that Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar lived for many years, making pilgrimages to many countries. However, from the Venbas he composed that are now available, we can see that Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar mostly visited holy places in the Chola and Thondai regions. The 18th Venba he composed is "Unjenai Magazham." This shows that he traveled beyond the borders of Tamil Nadu and worshipped Lord Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain in the north. We also see that he composed a Venba for that holy place. Unjenai Magazham refers to the Mahakaleshwar temple in Ujjain. This was the capital of Vikramaditya. It is a holy place where Lord Shiva, worshipped by Kali, resides, and is known as Mahakalam. Thus, it is understood that he worshipped beyond the borders of Tamil Nadu. There are also general verses, not specific to any holy place, which are the 10th and 23rd verses. These verses are composed in a simple and sweet style, understandable to everyone. They possess a gentle rhythm and an elevated flow, with various rasas (sentiments) such as word-flavor, meaning-flavor, humor, and grandeur. For those who read and practice them, they effectively convey the intended benefit. Now, we will continue to contemplate the 24 verses in the Kshetra Thiruvendha composed by Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar and their brief meanings. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 979, Poiyadimaiyilladha Pulavar Sarukkam, Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar Puranam, composed by divine Sekkizhar Peruman. We saw that Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar visited various holy places and composed one Venba for each, which was compiled as a work called Kshetra Thiruvendha in the 11th Thirumurai. Now, we will see that the 24 Venbas in this work, and their meanings, mostly speak of the impermanence of this body, the impermanence of wealth, and other transient aspects of this world. Therefore, this work is an instruction from Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar to visit holy places and worship the Lord. In that vein, the first Venba is for Thiruthillai Chitrambalam: "Odungindra neermai olithalume utraarum kodugindraar mooppum kurugitru naadugindra nalla Chitrambalame nanna munne nannanjche Thillai Chitrambalame ser." As long as our body is healthy and youthful, we run around and go everywhere. But when that state of being passes and old age sets in, even our relatives will not come forward to help us. When we are unable to move and are confined to one place, even close relatives and friends will distance themselves from us. Their faces and hearts will show displeasure. After our teeth fall out and we can no longer do anything with this body, when old age arrives, O good heart, before that old age comes, reach Thiruchitrambalam in Thillai. If you reach the Lord who performs the sacred dance in Thillai Thiruchitrambalam, He will free us from the troubles of birth and death. This verse is an instruction to his own heart, and in that sense, it serves as an instruction to the world. The second Venba is for Thirukudanthai Keezhkottam: "Kaveri thenkarai endru nadunaduththu naavadanga munnam podiyeduththa paal kottam seramun panmaada thenkudanthai keezhkottam seppikkida." In old age, before your tongue trembles and you can no longer speak, expecting others to bring water and medicine, and before you join the ash-filled cremation ground, praise and reside in Thirukeezhkottam in Thenkudanthai. Worship and praise Lord Nageshwarar at Thirukeezhkottam in Kudanthai. The third verse is for Thiruvaiyaru: "Kunthi nadandhu kunindhu oru kaikoal oondri nondhu irungi theri vandhu thodarnthu nadappatharku valimai illathathanaal adikkadi utkaarndhu utkaarndhu selgindra nilai udal valaindhu koonkondu oondru koal kondu nadagindra nilai koalinaal thaangum thunaiyindri indha udal thaangi nadakka iyalatha nilai irumal kondu varundhi muyandru utkaatrinaal valindhu thallappattu koazhai endra kabam adhu vaayin vazhiye aaru poala paayvatharku munne Thiruvaiyattrinai azhaithukonde iru paadikkonde iru Aiyaattrinai azhai adhanai vaayaal azhai vaai kettupovatharku munbe Aiyaattrinai azhai endru Nayanar arulugindraar." When you walk bent over, leaning on a stick, tired and unable to walk continuously, sitting down frequently, your body hunched, unable to walk without the support of a stick, before coughs rack you, and phlegm flows like a river from your mouth, call out Thiruvaiyaru. Keep singing Thiruvaiyaru. Call out Aiyaru with your mouth before your mouth fails you. The Nayanar instructs this. The Thevaram says that you don't even need to call out the Lord of Thiruvaiyaru; just calling out the name of the holy place Thiruvaiyaru is enough. So, this verse advises you to call out Aiyaru before you stop functioning. Thiruvaiyaru is the 51st holy place on the north bank of the Kaveri. Now, the fourth Venba is for Thiruvallur: "Kaalai vadi ozhindhu kaiyuravu odayura vaai naalum anugi naliyaamun paalai avizhmugam poonjolai aarurarku aalaai kavizhmugam koombuga en kai." Before the youthful form like a bull departs and you suffer daily with pain, before the spathes of the areca palm open, become a servant to the Lord of Thiruvarur, who resides in flower gardens. Let my face bow down and my hands fold in worship. Thiruvarur is the 87th holy place in the Chola country, on the south bank of the Kaveri. The fifth Venba is for Thiruthuruthi: "Vanji yana nunnidaiyaar vaazhthadanga neer soara kunji kurangin mel kondirundhu kanji aruththoru ththi kondu vaa ennaa mun nenchche Thiruthuruthiyaan paadham ser." In extreme old age, when women place you on their laps and tell another woman to bring gruel, and before that time comes, reach the feet of the Lord residing in Thiruthuruthi. Thiruthuruthi is the 37th holy place on the south bank of the Kaveri. The sixth Venba is for Thirukodikkaa, the 37th holy place on the north bank of the Kaveri: "Kaalai kalaiyizhayaal katti than kaiyaarththu maalai thalaikkanindhu maiyezhuthi melaor parukodi moodi palaramunna nee sendru kai peruviralgalai ivatraiyellam thuniyai kondu kattuvargal thalaikku maalai anindhu kangalukku maiyezhuthi udalin mele oru periya kodi thuniyinaal moodi palarum azhuvatharku munne nee sendru Thirukodikkaavinai adaiyavaayaga." Before they tie your feet with a cloth, cross your hands, adorn your head with a garland, apply kohl to your eyes, cover your body with a large cloth, and many people weep, you go and reach Thirukodikkaa. The seventh Venba is for Thiru Idaivai: "Maandu vaai angaavaamunnam madanenjche venduvaai aagi virainthollai paandavaai then nadai vaaymeya sivanaar thirunaamam ninidaivaai vaithu ninai." Before your mouth opens in death, O foolish heart, quickly desire and place the sacred name of Lord Shiva, who resides in Thiru Idaivai, within your heart and meditate on it. The eighth Venba is for Thirunedungalam: "Thottu thadavi thudippondrum kaanaadhu petta pinamendru peyarittu katti edungalaththa ennaa mun ezhai madanenjche nedungalaththaan paadham ninai." Before they touch and feel, finding no pulse, and declare it a mere corpse, saying "tie it up and take it away," O poor foolish heart, meditate on the feet of the Lord residing in Thirunedungalam. The ninth Venba is for Kuzhithandalai, the holy place of Thirukadambanthurai: "Azhagu thiri kurambai aangadhu vittu aavi ozhugum pozhudhu ariya onna kazhugu kazhiththundu alaiyaamun Kaaviriyin thenpaal Kuzhithandalaiyaanai kooru." It is impossible to know when the soul will leave this beautiful but decaying body. Before vultures devour and scatter your body, speak of the Lord of Kuzhithandalai, who resides on the south bank of the Kaveri, Lord Kadambavaneswarar. The tenth Venba is a general verse: "Padi muzhudhum venkudai keezh paar ellam aanda mudiyarasaar selvaththu mumbai kadiyilangu thodhendhu konraiyandhaar soathikku thonduppattu oadhendhi unpadhurum ulagam ellam." To serve Lord Shiva, who is a luminous being adorned with a garland of fragrant Konrai flowers, and to eat by begging with a begging bowl, is far superior to the wealth of crowned kings who ruled the entire world under their white parasols. The eleventh Venba is for Thiruvanaikka: "Kuzhiyirundha sutram gunangal paaraatta vazhiyirundha angangalellam thazhiyirundhum en aanaikkaava idhu thagaadhu." When a person is dying, relatives gather around, distressed, and praise the good qualities of the dying person. They embrace and arrange the limbs that have become limp. Before relatives lament, saying, "How could this happen to one like our elephant?" you go and reach the beautiful Thiruvanaikka. The twelfth Venba is for Thiru Mayilai Kuyilaththu: "Irul kunji kokkaththu irumal payila pugaamunnam nenchche Mayilai thiru punnaiyangaanal sindhiyaayaga iruppinnai angaandhu izhaithu kuyilin niram poandru irunda kariya thalaimuyir kokkin niram poandru naraithu vellai mayiraagavum irumal migundhu varavum ulla mooppu paruvam varuvatharku munbe Thirumayilaappoorin kan ulla Thiru Punnaiyangaanal pathiyinai ninaikka maattaay endraar." Before your dark hair, like the color of a cuckoo, turns white like a crane, and before severe coughing sets in, O heart, will you not think of the sacred Punnaiyangaanal in Thirumayilappur? The thirteenth Venba is for Thiru Unjenai Magazham. This is Mahakaleshwar, a Jyotirlinga temple in Madhya Pradesh, formerly the capital of Malwa. It is one of the twelve Jyotirlinga holy places. "Kaalaiyargal eezhaiyargalaagi karumayirum poozhaiyena pongi polivizhந்து soozhaiyargal o kaalam seyyaamun nenchame Unjenai Magaakaalam kaithozhudhu vaazhthu." Before young men become sickly, losing their vitality, their black hair turning white like the cotton flower, and before friends sigh and weep, O heart, worship and praise Mahakalam in Unjenai with folded hands. The fourteenth Venba is for Thiruvalaikoottam: "Illum porulum irundha manaiyalave sollum ayalaar thudippalave nalla kilaikkulaththu neeralave kittriye nenchche valaikkulaththul eesanaiye vaazhthu." This verse describes the state of affairs when the body is taken to the cremation ground after the soul departs. This verse is similar to Pattinathar's unique poem, "Aththamum vaazhvum agaththu matte vizhi ambu ozhuga meththiya maadharum veedhi matte vimmivi simmi iru kaiththalam mel vaiththu azhum maindharum sudakaadu matte patti thodarum iruvinai punniya paavamume." The wife and other possessions accompany only up to the house. Some relatives, after following the corpse for a short distance, turn back without staying until the end. Some relatives go all the way, immerse themselves in water, and then return. Even so, no one will ever accompany the soul. If you realize all this, O mind, praise only the Lord residing in Thiruvanaikoottam. The fifteenth Venba is for Thirusaikkadu: "Anjananjer kannaar arurukkum appadhamai kunji veluththu udalam kodaamun nenchame poikaadu kooda pulambaadhu poompugaar Saikkaadu kaithozhu nee saarndhu." Before the women with kohl-lined eyes reach old age, before your hair turns white and your body bends, before you go to the forest (cremation ground), O heart, without lamenting, join and worship Thirusaikkadu in Poompuhar with folded hands. The sixteenth Venba is for Thirupachilachiramam: "Itta kudineer irunaali oruzhakaaga satta oru muttai ney thaan kalandhu aatta aruvaacharendrangu azhaamunnaachil Thiru Aachiramame ser." In old age, when medicine is given, two measures of water are boiled down to one measure, and a small spoonful of ghee is mixed in and administered. Even with all this care, before others weep, saying that the life has departed, reach Thirupachilachiramam. The seventeenth verse is for Thiruchirappalli: "Kazhindhadhu nintrukku attuvittu naari olindhu udal iraavannam azhindhadhiramaalaiaa kondu po ennaa mun nenchche Siramalaiyaan paadhame ser." When the body, like dry straw, is discarded and emits a foul odor, making it unbearable to stay, before they say, "Take this decaying body away," O heart, reach the feet of the Lord of Thirusiramalai. The eighteenth Venba is for Thirumazhapadi: "Izhavaadi sutrathaar ellaarum koodi vizhavaadi aavi vidaamunnam Mazhapaadi aandaanai aar amudhai andru ayanmaal kaanaamai neendaanai nenchche ninai." Before all the relatives gather, lamenting the loss, and before the soul leaves the body, O heart, meditate on the Lord who rules Thirumazhapadi, who is like abundant nectar, and who stood tall, beyond the sight of Brahma and Vishnu. The nineteenth Venba is for Thiruvappadi: "Ullidaththaan vallaiye nenchame oozhvinigal kallidaththaan vandhu kalavaamun Kollidaththin then Thiruvappadiyaan deiva marai naanginaiyum vandhu kalandhu kondu povatharku munbe." Before the karmic deeds come and mix with you, O heart, you must meditate on the feet of the Lord who resides in Thiruvappadi on the south bank of the Kollidam, and who sang the four divine Vedas with his sacred mouth. The twentieth Venba is for Kachi Thiruekambam: "En nenchche unnai irandhum uraikkindren kannanjeyvaayagil kaalaththaal vannanjey se ma kambaththaanai uriththaanai van Kachi Ekambaththaanai irainju." O my heart, listen, I implore you. If you wish to be saved, worship the Lord of Kachi Ekambam, who flayed the mighty elephant, during the time your soul is still in your body. The twenty-first Venba is for Thiruppanandal Thadakeswaram: "Karam oondri kan idungi matror maram oondri vaaykuthathaamunnam puramoonrum theechcharaththaal setraan Thiruppanandhaal Thadakeechcharaththaan paadhame eththu." Before you lean on your hand, your eyes sink, you lean on another support stick, and your mouth becomes unable to speak, praise the feet of Lord Shiva, who burned the three cities with a fiery arrow, the Lord residing in Thadakeswaram in Thiruppanandal. The twenty-second Venba is for Thiruottiyur: "Thanjaaga moovulagam aandu thalaiyaliththuttu enjaamai petridanum yaan vendan nanjam karandhundha kandardham Ottiyoor patti irandhu undu irukka perin." I do not desire to rule the three worlds as a refuge and receive endless blessings, if I can live by begging and eating in Thiruottiyur, clinging to the Lord with the dark throat who swallowed poison. The twenty-third Venba is a general verse: "Nootranaitthor palloozhi venkudai keezh veetrirundha selvam vilaiyaadhe kootruththaan aadaravam kachcha araikkasaiththa ammaandhan paadaravam ketta pagal." A single day spent listening to the sacred song (Vedic hymns) of Lord Shiva, who kicked Yama and tied a dancing serpent as a belt around his waist, is superior to the wealth gained by ruling under a white parasol for hundreds of thousands of eons. The twenty-fourth Venba is for Thirumayanam, which is considered Thirukadavur Mayanam: "Uyir marundhidhanai unmin ena utraar kaiyai pidiththu edhire kaattiya kaal paiya ezhundhu irumi yaan vendan ennaa mun nenchche sezhunthirumayanam ser." When relatives hold your hand and show you a medicine, saying, "Eat this life-saving medicine," before you slowly rise, cough, and say, "I don't want it," O heart, reach the prosperous Thirumayanam. The mention of "Sezhumayanam" (prosperous cremation ground) here, distinguishing it from other cremation grounds, indicates that this Mayanam is not a degrading burial ground where people go after falling ill and dying. It is a prosperous Mayanam, a place of imperishable liberation and joy where the Lord resides, which is Thirukadavur Thirumayanam. Thus concludes the Kshetra Thiruvendha. Thiruchitrambalam. Thus concludes the Puranam of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar. Now, the concluding and eighth verse of this Puranam is: "Paiyarava maniyaaram anindhaarkku paavanindha Iyadigal Kadavanaar adi inaiththaamarai vanangi kaiyanimaan mazhu udaiyaar kadal pani sindhanai udaiya seythar thiruththondu viriththu uraippaam." Worshipping the lotus feet of Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar, who adorned the Lord with Venbas like a garland of serpents, we shall now elaborate on the sacred service of Kanampulla Nayanar, who possessed the thought of serving the Lord with the deer and axe in his hands. With this, divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes this Puranam and proceeds to explain the sacred history of Kanampulla Nayanar, the next Puranam in the next Sarukkam. At this point, the Puranams of the Nayanars in this Sarukkam are concluded. Now, the concluding line of the seventh verse of Thiruthondar Thogai is: "Arooran Arooril ammaanakku aale." This means, "I, Arooran, am a servant to the Lord of Thiruvarur, in addition to being a servant to all the devotees mentioned before." In accordance with this, Nambiandar Nambigal has composed the praise of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, which is the 57th verse: "Pallavai sengkathironai pariththu avan paadham pugazh sollavan then pugazh Arooran paal thuyya sempon kolla vallava naatti." This means that Nambi Aroorar, who praises the feet of Lord Shiva who removed the sun's rays, and who received pure gold in the form of red bricks from the Lord of beautiful Thiruppukalur, and who is devoted to Lord Manikkavannathar, who resides in Thirunattiyaththangudi, hails from the good city of Thirunavalur. Now, in the Viri Nool, the Periya Puranam, we see that divine Sekkizhar Peruman includes the praise of Sundaramoorthy Nayanar at the end of each Sarukkam. In that vein, at the end of this Sarukkam of Periya Puranam, the Poiyadimaiyilladha Pulavar Sarukkam, the 4054th verse contains the praise of Sundaramoorthy Nayanar, which is dedicated to this Sarukkam. That verse is as follows: "Ullaththil oru thulakkam illavum ulagavuyy irunda thirukalaththar mudukuntrar tharu kanagam aattrinil ittu valaththin malindhu ezhvulagam vanangu perum Thiruvaroor kulaththil eduththaar vinaiyin kuzhi vaai nindru ena eduththaar." The Lord with the dark throat who swallowed poison to save the world, the Lord of Thirumudukunram, gave gold. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, also known as Nambi Aroorar, put that gold into the Thirumanimuththar River and later retrieved it from the Kamalalayam pond in Thiruvarur, which is worshipped by seven worlds. He lifted me from the deep pit of karma. Therefore, there is no tremor in my heart. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal retrieved gold from the Kamalalayam pond in Thiruvarur. Not only that, he also lifted us from the pit of birth, which deeply immerses those who commit karma. Therefore, there is no tremor in my mind due to the three impurities. Thus, divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. At this point, the eight Puranams in the eighth Sarukkam, the Poiyadimaiyilladha Pulavar Sarukkam, are concluded, and the eighth Sarukkam itself is concluded. Now, we will continue to contemplate the ninth Sarukkam, Karaikandan Sarukkam, beginning with the first Puranam, Kanampulla Nayanar Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam. Praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Iyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar! Praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal! Praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman! Praise to the sacred feet of divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Thiruchitrambalam.
