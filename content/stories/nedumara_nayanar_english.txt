Chapter 01 — The Hunchback King

The Pandyan land was ruled from Madurai by King Nedumaran. Because his back was hunched, the people called him Koon Pandiyan, the hunchbacked Pandyan.

In those days Jainism had grown powerful in the Pandyan country. Heeding the Jain monks, the king himself embraced their faith, and Shaivism began to decline in the land.

Chapter 02 — The Queen and the Minister

The king's queen, Mangayarkkarasiyar, was a Chola princess and an ardent devotee of Lord Shiva. His prime minister, Kulachchiraiyar, was also a steadfast servant of Shiva.

Seeing the Jain ascendancy in the palace, the queen and the minister grieved and sought a way to redeem Shaivism. Just then they heard of the boy-saint Gnanasambandar of Seerkazhi and the miracles flowering around him.

Chapter 03 — The Invitation

At the queen's bidding, Minister Kulachchiraiyar invited Gnanasambandar to grace Madurai. The saint accepted and came, lodging in a hospice outside the city.

Learning of his arrival, the Jains plotted to destroy him and set fire to the hospice where he stayed that night.

Chapter 04 — Fire in the Hospice

Seeing the hospice ablaze, Sambandar knew it for Jain work. He sang "Payyave senru Pandiyarkkahave" — let the fire go softly and settle upon the Pandyan — turning the heat of the flames toward the king.

The next morning the king's body broke out in burning boils. Jain medicines and mantras failed; when they stroked him with peacock feathers, the burning only grew worse.

Chapter 05 — Healing with Sacred Ash

Sambandar came to the king, smeared him with holy vibhuti, and sang "Mandiramavadhu Neeru" — the mantra is the sacred ash. The burning fever left him, and with it his hunched back straightened.

All marvelled to see the king standing straight and tall. From that day he was hailed as Ninra Seer Nedumaran — Nedumaran who stood erect. His mind cleared, the king returned to Shaivism.

Chapter 06 — Fire and Water

The Jains called Sambandar's cure magic and demanded ordeals, the loser to be impaled. First the Anal Vadham, ordeal by fire: of the palm-leaves cast into the flames, Sambandar's Tirunallaru hymn-leaf came out green and fresh, while the Jains' leaf was reduced to ash.

Then the Punal Vadham, ordeal by water: cast into the Vaigai river, Sambandar's "Vazhga Andhanar" leaf floated upstream to Tiruvedagam, while the Jains' leaf sank. Sambandar had won.

Chapter 07 — The Shaivite King

Restored to Shaivism, the king honoured Shiva's temples across the land. When northern kings invaded, he defeated them in battle at Tirunelveli and guarded the country.

Queen and minister worshipped Sambandar as their guru with steadfast devotion. The Meenakshi Sundareswarar temple of Madurai shone again with Shaivite light.

Chapter 08 — Among the Sixty-Three

Nedumaran's change of heart and lifelong service to Shaivism earned him a place among the Nayanmars — as it did for Queen Mangayarkkarasiyar and Minister Kulachchiraiyar beside him.

Sekkizhar sings this history as the Ninra Seer Nedumara Nayanar Puranam in the Karaikandan Sarukkam of the Periyapuranam. It is cherished to this day as the story of darkness lifting and Shaivism returning.
